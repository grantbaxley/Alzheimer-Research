# State of Evidence (as of Oct 2026): Preventing, Slowing, and Reversing Alzheimer's Disease

Evidence-grading key used throughout:
- **A (strong)** = replicated, adequately powered RCT(s) on a clinical endpoint.
- **B (moderate)** = single positive RCT, or RCT positive only on biomarkers / pre-specified subgroup, or strong quasi-experimental (natural experiment) data.
- **C (preliminary)** = small/open-label/phase 1-2 trials, observational associations, mechanistic human data.
- **D (hype / unsupported)** = case series, uncontrolled reports, animal-only data marketed as human benefit.

Sourcing note: items marked "(primary paper; not re-fetched this session)" cite well-known primary publications whose figures come from the researcher's background knowledge and were not re-verified by a fetch during this session. The report writer should treat those as high-confidence but check them if a figure is load-bearing.

**Skeptical stance (user requirement): independent vetting is required before anything here is used.** Each section ends with a per-claim grade table that records study design (RCT vs observational), replication, effect size vs clinical meaningfulness, conflicts of interest (COI), and critiques or retractions. Standing red flags:
1. **Sponsor-run trials.** Nearly every drug and device trial here was funded, run, and first reported by the company that profits from it. Topline press releases (CELIA, trontinemab, Cognito, Alzheon, Anavex) come before peer review and often lead with secondary or subgroup results when the primary endpoint failed.
2. **Biomarker ≠ benefit.** Amyloid or tau lowering has repeatedly failed to translate into clinical benefit: semaglutide improved biomarkers and failed; the diranersen dose-response was inverted.
3. **Compromised foundational literature.** The Lesné Aβ*56 Nature paper was retracted in 2024. The NIH found misconduct by Masliah, and Science flagged suspect images in 132 of his papers. Simufilam rested on work by an indicted academic collaborator and failed in Phase 3 (details in section 3). Any mechanism, target, or model parameter that traces back to these labs needs re-derivation from independent data.
4. **"Statistically significant" ≠ "clinically meaningful".** Approved antibodies slow decline by about 0.45-0.67 CDR-SB points over 18 months. The meaningful threshold is debated (often cited as ~1-2 points for MCI/mild AD; not sourced this session).
5. **Commercial sellers of "reversal".** The proponents of ReCODE (Bredesen / Apollo Health) and the Ornish program also run commercial programs (background knowledge; check the papers' COI statements).

## 1. Approved disease-modifying therapies (DMTs), real effect sizes, risks, and 2025-2026 readouts

### Takeaway
Two anti-amyloid antibodies (lecanemab, donanemab) are the only therapies with Grade A evidence of slowing clinical decline, and only modestly: roughly 0.45-0.67 CDR-SB points over 18 months (about 25-35% relative slowing). Neither stops or reverses the disease, and both carry ARIA risk that depends strongly on APOE4 status. Through Oct 2026 the main changes were in delivery and safety (subcutaneous lecanemab, donanemab titration). New mechanisms mostly failed (oral semaglutide in EVOKE/EVOKE+, posdinemab, blarcamesine at EMA, valiltramiprosate's primary endpoint). The one new mechanistic signal is tau antisense (diranersen/BIIB080), which is Grade C: it missed its primary endpoint.

### Cited Findings

**Lecanemab (Leqembi), Grade A, modest effect**
- In CLARITY AD (n≈1,795, early AD), the CDR-SB difference vs placebo at 18 months was −0.45 points (about 27% relative slowing). ARIA-E occurred in 12.6% vs 1.7% on placebo — [van Dyck et al., NEJM 2023](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2212948) (primary paper; not re-fetched this session). A review confirms the 0.45 CDR-SB figure for CLARITY-AD vs 0.67 for donanemab in TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 2 — [PMC review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11963282/). A secondary summary cites "27% CDR-SB reduction" — [Patsnap](https://www.patsnap.com/resources/blog/articles/lecanemab-vs-donanemab-vs-aducanumab-compared/) (low-authority source).
- ARIA-E in CLARITY-AD depends heavily on APOE4 genotype: 5.4% in non-carriers, 10.9% in heterozygotes, 32.6% in homozygotes — [PMC table](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12184013/table/tbl0003).
- Subcutaneous formulation: FDA approved Leqembi subcutaneous (IQLIK) for maintenance on Aug 29, 2025, and approved a subcutaneous *starting* dose on Jul 13, 2026. Treatment can now be fully at-home, starting either with weekly at-home injections or with IV every two weeks — [ADDF announcements](https://www.alzdiscovery.org/news-room/announcements); [Alzheimer's Association](https://www.alz.org/news/2026/alzheimers-association-welcomes-fda-approval-leqembi-subcutaneous-starter-dose).

**Donanemab (Kisunla), Grade A, modest effect, higher ARIA**
- TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 2: the CDR-SB difference vs placebo at 18 months was 0.67 points — [PMC review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11963282/). The trial reported about 35% slowing on iADRS in the low/medium-tau population, ARIA-E of about 24% vs about 2% on placebo, and treatment-associated deaths — [Sims et al., JAMA 2023](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2807533) (primary paper; not re-fetched this session). The "35% iADRS" figure also appears in [Patsnap](https://www.patsnap.com/resources/blog/articles/lecanemab-vs-donanemab-vs-aducanumab-compared/) (low-authority source).
- Modified titration (TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 6) lowered ARIA-E at 24 weeks to 13.7% vs 23.7% with standard dosing. Dose-skipping gave 18.6% and the Cmax arm 18.3%. The change shifts one vial from the first infusion to the third — [UKDRI summary](https://ukdri.ac.uk/publications/modified-titration-donanemab-reduces-aria-risk-and-maintains-amyloid-reduction); [Lilly release](https://investor.lilly.com/node/51501).
- Donanemab was authorized in the EU, with reported sustained benefit over 3 years in the long-term extension and less serious ARIA under revised dosing. That source gives no figures — [Patient Care Online, Year in AD](https://www.patientcareonline.com/view/the-year-in-alzheimer-disease-disease-modifying-therapies-regulatory-approvals-and-safety); see also [TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 2 LTE, PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12869028/).

**Aducanumab (Aduhelm), withdrawn**
- Aducanumab is no longer marketed; one source calls it "a recently discontinued treatment" — [NeurologyLive](https://www.neurologylive.com/view/combination-ultrasound-blood-brain-barrier-opening-aducanumab-significantly-reduces-amyloid-ad). (Background: Biogen discontinued it in Jan 2024 after a contested 2021 accelerated approval. Not re-verified this session.)

**Next-generation anti-amyloid antibodies (no Phase 3 efficacy data yet)**
- Trontinemab (Roche; Brainshuttle, a transferrin-receptor BBB shuttle): in Phase 1b/2a, 92% of patients on 3.6 mg/kg fell below the amyloid-positivity threshold at 28 weeks. ARIA-E was under 5% across the 1.8 and 3.6 mg/kg cohorts. These are small-study, biomarker-only results — [Clinical Trials Arena, AAIC 2026](https://www.clinicaltrialsarena.com/news/roche-alzheimers-trontinemab-aaic-26/); [NeurologyLive](https://www.neurologylive.com/view/roche-unveils-preventron-phase-3-prevention-trial-trontinemab-cognitively-unimpaired-individuals-high-risk-alzheimers); [Brainshuttle AD Part 2, PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12740976/).
- The trontinemab Phase 3 trials TRONTIER 1 and 2 are expected to report in 2028 — [BrightFocus 2026 forecast](https://www.brightfocus.org/resource/expanding-the-alzheimers-treatment-landscape-a-2026-forecast/). PrevenTRON, a new Phase 3 prevention trial (~1,600 cognitively unimpaired people aged 55-80, CDR 0), was unveiled at AAIC 2026 with time to clinical progression as its primary endpoint — [NeurologyLive](https://www.neurologylive.com/view/roche-unveils-preventron-phase-3-prevention-trial-trontinemab-cognitively-unimpaired-individuals-high-risk-alzheimers).
- Remternetug (Lilly): in a Phase 1 MAD study, 75% (18/24) of participants on 700-2800 mg every 4 weeks reached amyloid clearance below 24.1 CL by day 169. TRAILRUNNER-ALZ 3 is a Phase 3 trial of subcutaneous dosing in early AD — [NeurologyLive](https://www.neurologylive.com/view/phase-3-trailrunner-alz-3-test-effects-igg1-monoclonal-antibody-remternetug-early-alzheimer-disease). No TRAILRUNNER-ALZ 1 efficacy results were found.

**Tau-targeting**
- Diranersen (BIIB080; intrathecal tau antisense, Biogen/Ionis), Phase 2 CELIA: the trial **did not meet its primary endpoint** (dose response). All doses produced robust reductions in tau pathology. Pre-specified cognitive analyses showed slower decline at all doses, with the strongest effect at the *lowest* dose (60 mg every 24 weeks). The highest dose had more serious adverse events. Biogen plans registrational development. Topline was May 14, 2026, with details at AAIC on Jul 14, 2026 — [Biogen](https://investors.biogen.com/news-releases/news-release-details/topline-results-phase-2-celia-study-diranersen-biib080-first); [Alzheimer's Association](https://www.alz.org/news/2026/phase-2-results-celia-clinical-trial-tau-targeting-therapy-diranersen); [Ionis AAIC poster PDF](https://ionis.com/sites/default/files/2026-07/Mummery%20et%20al%20-%20Topline%20Results%20from%20CELIA%20-%20A%20Phase%202%20Study%20to%20Evaluate%20the%20Tau-Targeting%20ASO%20Diranersen%20(BIIB080)%20in%20Patients%20with%20Early%20Alzheimer%20Disease.pdf). ADDF called this the "first evidence that targeting tau may slow cognitive decline" while noting that the best signal came at the lowest dose — [ADDF](https://www.alzdiscovery.org/news-room/announcements).
- Posdinemab (J&J anti-tau antibody) failed its primary endpoint in Phase 2b AuTonomy and the trial was halted — [Patient Care Online](https://www.patientcareonline.com/view/the-year-in-alzheimer-disease-disease-modifying-therapies-regulatory-approvals-and-safety).

**GLP-1: semaglutide EVOKE / EVOKE+ (negative, Grade A null)**
- Two Phase 3 trials (n=3,808 total, aged 55-85, MCI or mild dementia due to AD) did not show that oral semaglutide beats placebo on CDR-SB. AD biomarkers improved, but "this did not translate into a delay in disease progression." The 1-year extension was discontinued. Topline came Nov 24, 2025, with presentation at CTAD in Dec 2025 — [Alzheimer Europe](https://www.alzheimer-europe.org/news/results-evoke-and-evoke-trials-show-no-effect-oral-semaglutide-ad-progression); [Novo Nordisk via BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/press-releases/novo-nordisk-a-s-evoke-phase-3-trials-did-not-demonstrate-a-statistically-significant-reduction-in-alzheimers-disease-progression); [NeurologyLive CTAD](https://www.neurologylive.com/view/ctad-presentation-lays-insights-disappointing-phase-3-evoke-trial-of-glp-1-semaglutide-alzheimers).
- ADDF still argues that GLP-1s may have a role in prevention or combination therapy. That is opinion, not evidence — [ADDF](https://www.alzdiscovery.org/news-room/announcements/readout-of-phase-3-semaglutide-trials-marks-critical-moment-in-alzheimers-research-and-suggests-potential-for-combination-therapies).

**Blarcamesine (Anavex; sigma-1 agonist), regulator-rejected**
- The EMA's CHMP gave a negative opinion in Dec 2025: the main study "failed to demonstrate the effectiveness and safety" in early AD patients without SIGMAR1 mutations — [Alzheimer Europe](https://www.alzheimer-europe.org/news/european-medicines-agency-issues-negative-opinion-blarcamesine?language_content_entity=en); [EMA CHMP highlights Dec 2025](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/news/meeting-highlights-committee-medicinal-products-human-use-chmp-8-11-december-2025). Anavex asked for re-examination — [StockTitan](https://www.stocktitan.net/news/AVXL/anavex-life-sciences-submitted-request-for-ema-to-re-examine-its-iaujxvkr02pq.html). One low-authority outlet reports that Anavex withdrew the application on Mar 25, 2026, which is unverified — [HelpDementia](https://helpdementia.com/anavex-eu-alzheimers-drug-bid-halted-after-ema-pushback/).

**Valiltramiprosate (ALZ-801; oral anti-oligomer, APOE4/4), primary endpoint missed**
- APOLLOE4 Phase 3 missed its primary endpoint (ADAS-Cog). A pre-specified mild-disease subgroup looked positive. The drug is not approved anywhere — [Alzheon topline](https://alzheon.com/topline-results-from-pivotal-apolloe4-phase-3-trial-of-oral-valiltramiprosate-alz-801-in-patients-with-early-alzheimers-disease-carrying-two-copies-of-apoe4-gene/). No FDA decision was found as of Oct 2026.

**Symptomatic (not disease-modifying) 2026 approval**
- On Apr 30, 2026, the FDA approved Auvelity (dextromethorphan-bupropion) for AD agitation, the first non-antipsychotic for this indication — [Alzheimer's Association](https://www.alz.org/news/2026/alzheimers-association-welcomes-fda-approval-treatment-for-agitation).

### Inferences
- The proven ceiling for DMTs is about 0.5-0.7 CDR-SB points over 18 months. That is statistically robust, but whether it is clinically meaningful is still debated (CDR-SB minimal clinically important difference estimates are often cited near 1-2 points; not sourced this session). No approved therapy halts or reverses decline.
- The pattern in 2025-2026 is that biomarker improvement does not reliably predict clinical benefit: semaglutide improved biomarkers and failed, and diranersen showed an inverse dose-cognition relationship. An open-source project should not treat biomarker change alone as proof of efficacy.
- Delivery (subcutaneous, BBB shuttle) and safety (titration, lower ARIA) are improving faster than efficacy.
- APOE4 homozygotes face the highest ARIA risk and the least favorable benefit/risk balance. Genotype-stratified risk modeling is a concrete, well-grounded goal.

## 2. Prevention trials and modifiable risk factors

### Takeaway
No drug has yet been proven to prevent AD in cognitively normal people. A4 (solanezumab) was negative. TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 3 and AHEAD 3-45 are still running, with readouts around 2027+. For lifestyle, the strongest evidence is now US POINTER (2025, JAMA; n=2,111). A structured multidomain program beat a self-guided one on global cognition, but the effect was modest and it measured cognition, not dementia incidence. The Lancet Commission's "~45% preventable" figure is a population-attributable-fraction model built on observational associations, not trial evidence. Hearing aids (ACHIEVE) were null overall. Shingles vaccination has unusually strong quasi-causal (natural-experiment) support.

### Cited Findings

**Anti-amyloid prevention trials**
- A4 (solanezumab in preclinical AD): no slowing of cognitive decline vs placebo over 240 weeks — [Sperling et al., NEJM 2023](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2305032) (primary paper; not re-fetched this session). Grade A null.
- TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 3 (donanemab, preclinical AD) is ongoing (active, not recruiting), with primary completion estimated Nov 2027. Its endpoint is time to clinical progression — [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05026866](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05026866). The trial screened 63,124 people in the US and Japan using plasma p-tau217 with a decentralized design. It enrolled 2,196: 1,202 at CDR-GS 0 and 664 at CDR-GS 0.5. Baseline amyloid was 63.2 CL and 70.7 CL respectively — [Yaari et al., Alzheimer's & Dementia 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12439032/). This is a key proof that blood-test-based prevention screening works at scale.
- AHEAD study (lecanemab in preclinical AD) is ongoing with no readout date given — [BrightFocus](https://www.brightfocus.org/resource/expanding-the-alzheimers-treatment-landscape-a-2026-forecast/).
- DIAN-TU (autosomal dominant AD): a Lancet Neurology study led by Bateman suggested that early anti-amyloid treatment may reduce risk in people destined to develop AD in their 30s-50s — [ADDF, Mar 20 2025](https://www.alzdiscovery.org/news-room/announcements). (Background, not verified: this was an open-label extension of a small gantenerumab subgroup, so Grade C.)
- Trontinemab PrevenTRON Phase 3 prevention trial (~1,600 people, CDR 0) — [NeurologyLive](https://www.neurologylive.com/view/roche-unveils-preventron-phase-3-prevention-trial-trontinemab-cognitively-unimpaired-individuals-high-risk-alzheimers).

**Modifiable risk factors: Lancet Commission 2024**
- 14 modifiable risk factors account for about 45% of dementia cases worldwide. The report was published Jul 31, 2024, led by Gill Livingston. Hearing loss contributes about 7% in the model. The 2020 version listed 12 factors and about 40% — [ACHIEVE/Commission summary, UNC & others](https://sph.unc.edu/?p=407322); [Lancet Commission 2024](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736(24)01296-0/fulltext) (primary report; not re-fetched this session). Background, not re-verified: the 14 factors are less education, hearing loss, high LDL cholesterol, depression, traumatic brain injury, physical inactivity, diabetes, smoking, hypertension, obesity, excess alcohol, social isolation, air pollution, and untreated vision loss. The last two newly added in 2024 were high LDL and vision loss.

**Multidomain lifestyle RCTs**
- US POINTER (JAMA, Jul 28, 2025): a 2-year RCT of 2,111 older adults at risk, at 5 US sites. Structured and self-guided multidomain programs (exercise, MIND diet, cognitive/social engagement, health monitoring) both improved cognition. The structured program did better, which the lead investigator described as delaying cognitive aging by "one to nearly two years over and above the self-guided group." Benefits held regardless of sex, ethnicity, APOE4 status, or heart health — [Alzheimer's Association](https://alz.org/us-pointer/study-results); [Wake Forest](https://newsroom.wakehealth.edu/news-releases/2025/07/groundbreaking-study-shows-lifestyle-changes-significantly-improve-brain-health); [UC Davis](https://health.ucdavis.edu/awards/about/us-pointer-study-shows-lifestyle-program-improves-cognition-in-older-adults/2025/07). Grade A-/B: a large, rigorous RCT, but the comparator was an active intervention, there was no no-intervention arm, and the outcome was a cognitive composite, not dementia incidence.
- FINGER (Finland, 2015): a 2-year multidomain intervention (n≈1,260) improved a cognitive composite vs control — [Ngandu et al., Lancet 2015](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736(15)60461-5/fulltext) (primary paper; not re-fetched this session).

**Hearing: ACHIEVE (null overall)**
- 977 adults aged 70-84 with untreated hearing loss were randomized to hearing aids plus counseling or to health education. There was little difference in cognitive outcomes overall. A 48% slowing appeared only in a pre-specified subgroup of 238 participants from the ARIC heart-study cohort, who declined about 3x faster. The healthy-volunteer group showed no effect — [UNC SPH](https://sph.unc.edu/?p=407322); [ENT Bulletin](https://bulletin.entnet.org/clinical-patient-care/article/22873554/hearing-loss-and-cognitive-decline-results-of-the-achieve-study); [Alzheimer Europe](https://www.alzheimer-europe.org/news/hearing-aids-may-reduce-cognitive-decline-older-adults-risk-dementia-according-new-findings). Grade B (subgroup) / null primary.

**Shingles vaccine (natural experiment)**
- Eyting et al., Nature 2025 (vol. 641, pp. 438-446) used Wales's birthdate-based eligibility cutoff (born on/after Sept 2, 1933 = eligible) as a regression-discontinuity natural experiment with >280,000 people. The live Zostavax vaccine reduced new dementia diagnoses over 7 years by 3.5 percentage points, a 20% relative reduction, with a stronger effect in women — [Stanford Medicine](https://med.stanford.edu/news/all-news/2025/03/shingles-vaccination-dementia); [Gavi](https://www.gavi.org/vaccineswork/vaccination-against-shingles-may-cut-dementia-risk); [Nature paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08800-x) (primary paper; not re-fetched). A separate 2024 Nature Medicine study suggested that the recombinant vaccine (Shingrix) may be associated with even greater protection — [Gavi](https://www.gavi.org/vaccineswork/vaccination-against-shingles-may-cut-dementia-risk). Grade B: strong quasi-causal design, but not an RCT, and the mechanism (antiviral vs immune) is unresolved.

### Inferences
- Primary prevention with drugs is unproven. The decisive readouts (TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 3 ~2027-28, AHEAD 3-45, PrevenTRON later) will define the field in the next 2-4 years.
- Lifestyle and vascular-risk management have the best risk/benefit ratio available today and the most RCT support for *cognitive* benefit. Evidence that they prevent *AD pathology* or *dementia diagnosis* is still weaker.
- The 45% figure should be presented as "potentially attributable under causal assumptions", not "preventable".
- Blood-test-enabled screening (TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 3's 63k screen) makes large decentralized prevention trials feasible. That is relevant to any open-source data or modeling project.

## 3. "Reversal" claims: Bredesen/ReCODE, Ornish 2024, case reports

### Takeaway
No intervention has RCT-level evidence of reversing established Alzheimer's disease. The Ornish 2024 trial is a small (n≈51), 20-week, unblinded, waitlist-controlled pilot with mixed results; it needs replication (Grade C). The Bredesen/ReCODE protocol rests on uncontrolled case series and is widely criticized by Alzheimer's organizations (Grade D). Anti-amyloid antibodies remove plaque, and that biomarker "reversal" does not reverse symptoms.

### Cited Findings
- Ornish et al. 2024 (Alzheimer's Research & Therapy): an intensive multimodal lifestyle intervention (plant-based diet, exercise, stress management, group support) improved some cognition and function measures after 20 weeks. Two dropouts were excluded from the analyses. Plasma Aβ42/40 improved 6.4% in the intervention group vs an 8.3% decline in controls — [NeurologyLive](https://neurologylive.com/view/intensive-multimodal-lifestyle-intervention-improves-cognition-function-randomized-controlled-trial); [Ornish et al. 2024](https://alzres.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s13195-024-01482-z) (primary paper; not re-fetched).
- Critiques (Grill & Gillen commentary, Alz Res Ther): it used a waitlist control rather than a placebo/attention control. The dose-response analysis pooled groups ("no longer a randomized trial"), so adherence may confound it. Twenty weeks is short. Some participants were unchanged or worsened. Replication is required — [Grill & Gillen commentary PDF](https://biomedcentral-alzres.publicaciones.saludcastillayleon.es/counter/pdf/10.1186/s13195-024-01621-6.pdf). Favorable coverage came from advocacy groups such as PCRM — [PCRM](https://www.pcrm.org/news/health-nutrition/lifestyle-changes-significantly-improve-cognition-and-function-early).
- The trial is listed as ongoing, not accepting new patients — [UCSF NCT04606420](https://clinicaltrials.ucsf.edu/trial/NCT04606420).
- Bredesen/ReCODE: a 25-patient *uncontrolled* proof-of-concept reported that 84% improved test scores after 9 months. Bredesen says an IRB rejected his RCT attempt. A larger ~72-person, 6-site trial was described as "in progress" in 2023, with no published results found — [Sarasota Magazine](https://www.sarasotamagazine.com/health-and-fitness/2023/01/alzheimers-recode-bredesen).
- The Alzheimer Society of Canada says the protocol "offers false hope" and "has not been proven," and cites concerns raised in Lancet Neurology and Theoretical Medicine and Bioethics — [Alzheimer Society of Canada](https://alzheimer.ca/en/whats-happening/news/bredesen-protocol-offers-false-hope-reversing-alzheimers-disease). Red Pen Reviews scored the book's scientific accuracy 1.7 (out of 4) — [Red Pen Reviews](https://www.redpenreviews.org/?p=1666).
- The original Bredesen case series (2014, "Reversal of cognitive decline: a novel therapeutic program", 10 patients) — [Aging (Albany NY)](https://www.aging-us.com/article/100690) (primary paper; not re-fetched).

#### Red flags: retracted, fraudulent, or compromised work in the AD field
- **Lesné / Ashe Aβ*56 (Nature 2006), retracted 2024.** A 2022 Science investigation (whistleblower Matthew Schrag) found apparently manipulated images. All co-authors except first author Lesné agreed to retract. Retraction Watch describes it as among the most-cited papers ever retracted (~2,500 citations; sources disagree on its exact rank). Senior author Ashe maintains the conclusions hold and published an iScience paper claiming to reproduce them. Independent researchers questioned whether Aβ*56 can be isolated as described at all. Lesné left the University of Minnesota (reported Feb 2025) — [Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvain_Lesn%C3%A9); [Minnesota Reformer](https://minnesotareformer.com/2025/02/07/alzheimers-researcher-alleged-to-have-doctored-images-is-leaving-umn/); [Revista Pesquisa FAPESP](https://revistapesquisa.fapesp.br/en/detours-in-the-labyrinth-of-alzheimers-disease/). Implication: the "specific soluble oligomer Aβ*56 drives memory loss" line of evidence is compromised. The broader amyloid hypothesis rests on independent genetic and clinical data (e.g., APP/PSEN mutations, antibody RCTs) and does not depend on this paper. That last point is the researcher's inference, but conflating the two is a common error in both directions.
- **Eliezer Masliah (former NIA neuroscience director).** On Sep 26, 2024 the NIH announced a research-misconduct finding: figures reused and relabeled across two papers. The same day, Science published a forensic dossier flagging suspect images from 1997-2023 in **132 papers**, including work behind drug candidates in Parkinson's and Alzheimer's trials (e.g., α-synuclein programs; specifics not verified this session) — [Fierce Biotech](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/research/head-national-institute-aging-neuroscience-division-eliezer-masliah-found-have-committed); [AHCJ](https://healthjournalism.org/blog/2024/11/reporter-uncovers-potential-data-manipulation-by-top-researcher/); [Practical Neurology](https://practicalneurology.com/news/nih-alleges-research-misconduct-by-prominent-neuroscientist-and-investigator-of-a-synuclein-in-pd-and-ad/2470601/). Implication: any mouse-model, neuropathology, or α-synuclein/AD co-pathology result co-authored by Masliah should be excluded from model parameterization unless independently replicated.
- **Simufilam (Cassava Sciences): failed and compromised.** Phase 3 RETHINK-ALZ missed its co-primary endpoints, and REFOCUS-ALZ (n=1,125) was stopped Nov 25, 2024. In Mar 2025 its topline missed all endpoints, and Cassava ended the AD program — [Fierce Biotech](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/biotech/cassava-stops-alzheimers-program-simufilam-fails-another-phase-3); [Healio](https://www.healio.com/news/psychiatry/20241203/simufilam-fails-to-meet-endpoints-in-phase-3-alzheimers-study-trial-discontinued). Academic collaborator Hoau-Yan Wang was indicted on Jun 28, 2024 for allegedly fabricating Western blot images in NIH grant applications — [BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/news/two-senior-cassava-employees-subjects-in-federal-probes-after-former-advisor-indicted-for-fraud). On Sep 26, 2024 the SEC settled with Cassava ($40M penalty), its ex-CEO Barbier ($175k), and Burns ($85k) over misleading claims about the Phase 2 trial, and Wang paid $50k without admitting or denying — [SEC press release 2024-151](https://sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2024-151). Cassava denies wrongdoing. Lesson: a heavily promoted Phase 2 "cognitive improvement" signal from a small company with an academic-collaborator data pipeline was entirely false-positive.
- **Generalizable red flags seen across these cases:** a single-lab mechanistic foundation; Western blot or image-based evidence without raw-data sharing; uncontrolled "improvement" in early-phase data; company press releases ahead of peer review; and a retail-investor or patient-advocacy hype cycle. Ongoing AD claims that share these features (e.g., blarcamesine's post-hoc framing, valiltramiprosate's "super responders", lithium orotate supplement marketing) deserve the same scrutiny.

### Inferences
- "Reversal" should not be a stated goal for an evidence-grounded project. The realistic goals are slowing progression, delaying onset, and symptomatic support.
- Multidomain lifestyle effects (US POINTER, FINGER, Ornish) all point in the same direction, but only the large, rigorous trials (US POINTER, FINGER) count as strong, and those show slowing or improvement in at-risk people, not reversal of AD dementia.

## 4. Experimental frontier approaches

### Takeaway
Everything here is Grade C or below. The most advanced is 40 Hz gamma sensory stimulation (Cognito Spectris): Phase 3 HOPE is fully enrolled, with the readout expected mid/Aug 2026 but not confirmed in sources found. Focused-ultrasound BBB opening is a 3-patient proof of concept. Lithium orotate is mouse-only plus human postmortem association data. Tau antisense has the best clinical-stage signal (section 1). TREM2, senolytics, APOE gene therapy, epigenetic reprogramming, and glymphatic approaches remain early or, for TREM2, have had a notable failure.

### Cited Findings

**40 Hz gamma sensory stimulation (Cognito Spectris)**
- HOPE pivotal trial: 670 participants, a randomized, double-blind, sham-controlled design at 70 US sites, with 12 months of daily home use. The primary endpoint is a composite of ADCS-ADL and MMSE. Readout was expected around Aug / mid-2026. The device is not FDA approved but has Breakthrough Device designation — [BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/press-releases/cognito-therapeutics-completes-enrollment-in-hope-pivotal-study-of-spectris-ad-therapy-for-the-treatment-of-patients-with-alzheimers-disease); [Cognito](https://www.cognitotx.com/clinical-studies); [BrightFocus](https://www.brightfocus.org/resource/the-hope-study-testing-a-non-invasive-light-and-sound-stimulation-treatment-for-alzheimers/).
- Predecessor OVERTURE (small feasibility study): the company reports a 76% reduction in MMSE decline, and an AAIC 2025 open-label extension reported 8.66 months of "time saved" on ADL. These are company-reported, non-pivotal data — [Clinical Trials Arena](https://www.clinicaltrialsarena.com/news/aaic25-cognito-neuromodulation-therapy-slows-decline-in-alzheimer-disease/); [MedPath](https://trial.medpath.com/news/cognito-therapeutics-spectris-ad-demonstrates-significant-delay-in-alzheimer-s-disease-progression-through-non-invasive-neuromodulation).
- Related: Sinaptica (TMS-type non-invasive brain stimulation) reported 44% slowing of decline in a late-2024 Phase 2 — [BrightFocus](https://www.brightfocus.org/resource/expanding-the-alzheimers-treatment-landscape-a-2026-forecast/) (small trial; Grade C).

**Focused ultrasound BBB opening**
- Rezai et al., NEJM Jan 2024: in 3 participants receiving aducanumab, treated regions showed on average 32% greater amyloid reduction than untreated regions at 6 months. The trial was open-label and single-site. Adverse events were mainly mild headaches. A lecanemab follow-on was planned, and no results were found — [NeurologyLive](https://www.neurologylive.com/view/combination-ultrasound-blood-brain-barrier-opening-aducanumab-significantly-reduces-amyloid-ad); [Alzheimer's News Today](https://alzheimersnewstoday.com/news/focused-ultrasound-may-help-alzheimers-treatments-reach-brain). Grade C.

**Lithium (Nature 2025, Aron/Yankner lab, Harvard)**
- Published Aug 6, 2025. Endogenous brain lithium was lower in the prefrontal cortex in MCI and AD, and amyloid plaques sequester lithium. Lithium orotate (which binds amyloid less) prevented and reversed AD pathology and memory loss in mice without toxicity in long-term treatment — [NIH Research Matters](https://www.nih.gov/news-events/nih-research-matters/lithium-levels-tied-alzheimers-disease-dementia); [Harvard Medical School](https://hms.harvard.edu/news/bruce-yankners-alzheimers-research-journey).
- No human trial results exist. Yankner says a dose-finding clinical trial is planned, and experts say it is premature to recommend lithium — [PBS NewsHour](https://pbs.org/newshour/science/4-things-to-know-about-a-new-study-on-lithium-and-alzheimers-disease); [Being Patient explainer](https://beingpatient.com/science-behind-lithium-for-alzheimers-explained/embed/). Grade C (human association) / D if marketed as a supplement cure.

**Tau antisense (BIIB080/diranersen)** — see section 1 (Grade C+: Phase 2 missed primary endpoint, positive secondary and biomarker signals).

**Other tau programs with FDA Fast Track:** BMS-986446 (anti-MTBR tau, Phase 2) and etalanetug (anti-tau) — [Patient Care Online](https://www.patientcareonline.com/view/the-year-in-alzheimer-disease-disease-modifying-therapies-regulatory-approvals-and-safety).

**Pipeline context:** the 2026 Cummings pipeline report signals a shift toward biology-driven targets — [ADDF](https://www.alzdiscovery.org/news-room/announcements). More than 180 active AD trials span 15 biological targets — [Patient Care Online](https://www.patientcareonline.com/view/the-year-in-alzheimer-disease-disease-modifying-therapies-regulatory-approvals-and-safety).

### Inferences
- If Spectris HOPE turns out positive, it would be the first non-drug, non-amyloid intervention with pivotal-trial evidence. Its status needs checking before the report is final.
- Lithium is the frontier finding most likely to be hyped (cheap, sold as a supplement). It should be clearly flagged as animal-stage.
- The lowest-dose-best signal for tau antisense and the semaglutide biomarker/clinical disconnect both argue for humility about mechanism-based predictions. A brain-modeling project could add value by modeling biomarker-to-clinical translation explicitly.

## 5. Blood biomarkers and the revised biological definition

### Takeaway
Blood biomarkers are now clinically real. The FDA cleared the first AD blood test (Fujirebio Lumipulse pTau217/Aβ42 ratio) on May 16, 2025, and C2N's PrecivityAD2 on Aug 20, 2026 (for patients as young as 40). Plasma p-tau217 was used to screen 63k people for a prevention trial. The 2024 Alzheimer's Association criteria define AD biologically (by biomarkers, including in asymptomatic people). That enables earlier intervention, but it is controversial because most biomarker-positive, cognitively normal people have no proven preventive treatment yet.

### Cited Findings
- On May 16, 2025, the FDA cleared the Lumipulse G pTau217/β-Amyloid 1-42 Plasma Ratio, the first blood test to aid AD diagnosis — [ADDF](https://www.alzdiscovery.org/news-room/announcements); [FDA press release](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-clears-first-blood-test-used-diagnosing-alzheimers-disease) (primary; not re-fetched). Background, not re-verified: it is indicated for adults 55+ with cognitive symptoms and showed high PPV/NPV vs PET/CSF, with some indeterminate results.
- On Aug 20, 2026, the FDA cleared C2N PrecivityAD2, the "first Alzheimer's blood test cleared for patients as young as 40" — [ADDF](https://www.alzdiscovery.org/news-room/announcements).
- Jul 30, 2025: new clinical guidelines moved toward replacing invasive tools (PET, lumbar puncture) with blood tests — [ADDF](https://www.alzdiscovery.org/news-room/announcements).
- Plasma p-tau217 and Aβ42/40 show high accuracy for confirming amyloid positivity, which supports DMT eligibility testing — [Patient Care Online](https://www.patientcareonline.com/view/the-year-in-alzheimer-disease-disease-modifying-therapies-regulatory-approvals-and-safety).
- TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 3 used plasma p-tau217 to screen 63,124 people and enroll 2,196 for a decentralized prevention trial — [Yaari et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12439032/).
- ADDF committed $50M to tau blood tests, multi-marker panels, and AI biomarkers in Mar 2026, and launched a $5M co-pathology biomarker partnership with MJFF in Jul 2026 — [ADDF](https://www.alzdiscovery.org/news-room/announcements).
- Revised criteria: the 2024 Alzheimer's Association workgroup ("Revised criteria for diagnosis and staging of Alzheimer's disease") defines AD by core biomarkers (Core 1: Aβ42/40, p-tau217, amyloid PET) independent of symptoms, with biological (A-T) and clinical staging — [Jack et al., Alzheimer's & Dementia 2024](https://alz-journals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/alz.13859) (primary; not re-fetched). Background: an International Working Group (Dubois et al., JAMA Neurology 2024) objected, arguing that biomarker-positive, cognitively normal people should be called "at risk," not diagnosed with AD.

### Inferences
- Cheap, scalable blood tests shift the bottleneck from detection to proven early intervention. Today, a positive test in an asymptomatic person leads to no approved treatment, only lifestyle and risk-factor management and trial enrollment.
- An open-source project could ground goals in: (a) biomarker-trajectory modeling (p-tau217 → amyloid/tau PET → clinical progression), (b) individual risk prediction combining biomarkers, APOE, and Lancet risk factors, and (c) ARIA risk modeling. All have real, available data and clinical relevance.
