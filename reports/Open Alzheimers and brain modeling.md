# Build on open pipelines, re-test every claim

A lot of open Alzheimer's modeling already exists, and the solid part of it is mostly infrastructure. The reliable pieces are permissively licensed, maintained, peer-reviewed tools: neuroimaging pipelines (ClinicaDL, FastSurfer, SynthSeg), data-driven progression models (pySuStaIn), general-purpose brain simulators (NEURON, TVB, NEST, Brian2), and atlases (siibra/Julich-Brain). Mechanistic disease models, "digital twins," foundation models, and AI repurposing agents are much weaker. Most were never independently replicated. Their only validation is a conference abstract or a press release, or their license blocks redistribution in a public repo. The data is gated too. Nearly every major cohort (ADNI, OASIS, AIBL, NACC, A4, UK Biobank, the controlled tier of the AD Knowledge Portal, ADSP, GNPC) needs an application and a data-use agreement (DUA), and individual-level rows from any of them cannot go into `grantbaxley/alzheimer-research`. The exceptions are SEA-AD's processed atlas and CC0 OpenNeuro data. The clinical evidence sets hard limits on the project's goals. Only two anti-amyloid antibodies have Grade A evidence of slowing decline, by about 0.45–0.67 CDR-SB points over 18 months. No drug has been shown to prevent AD in cognitively normal people. Nothing has been shown to reverse it. Several heavily promoted signals (oral semaglutide, simufilam, the retracted Aβ*56 work) failed or collapsed. The best use of a new open project is to be the rigorous layer this field lacks: reproducible re-implementations, explicit null models, synthetic benchmark data, leakage-proof evaluation, and provenance tracking. It should not be one more model claiming to cure the disease.

## Pipelines and progression models are mature; mechanistic and "twin" claims are not

The most reusable open work is in data-driven disease progression modeling. The UCL POND group's **pySuStaIn** (MIT, 222 stars, last push 2026-03-30) implements Subtype and Stage Inference. It is the most widely adopted tool in this layer ([GitHub](https://github.com/ucl-pond/pySuStaIn)), and its method underlies the Nature Medicine 2021 finding of four tau-PET trajectories ([Vogel et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-021-01309-6)). Replication is real but partial. A reproducibility study across eight cohorts recovered the three original atrophy subtypes in most of them, but extra clusters appeared, and excluding controls during fitting often made the Subcortical or Cortical subtype disappear ([arXiv 2412.00160](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.00160)). In an independent Knight ADRC cohort of 629 participants, analysis found **three tau subtypes rather than four** ([PMC12734641](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12734641/)). The Inria/Paris Brain Institute ecosystem adds **Leaspy** (BSD-3-Clause-Clear, pushed 2026-09-17) for individual-level trajectory modeling and simulation, plus **Clinica/ClinicaDL** for converting ADNI, AIBL, and OASIS to BIDS and running reproducible deep learning ([Leaspy](https://github.com/aramis-lab/leaspy); [ClinicaDL](https://github.com/aramis-lab/clinicadl)). Leaspy's own documentation says it "outperformed 56 alternative methods" in TADPOLE ([Leaspy docs](https://leaspy.readthedocs.io/en/dev/to_go_further.html)). The TADPOLE organisers' peer-reviewed summary of **92 algorithms from 33 teams**, however, found that **no method beat random guessing for ADAS-Cog13 forecasting**, and that simple ensembles topped most tasks ([arXiv 2002.03419](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.03419)). A ranking win is not clinically meaningful accuracy, and that gap between the two claims sets the tone for everything downstream.

Mechanistic modeling has one plug-and-play layer: connectome-based spreading models. These include the network diffusion model, Fisher-Kolmogorov (FKPP), amyloid-weighted FKPP, and SIR variants. Several groups support the concept. One study of 295 people found connectome spread far outperformed Euclidean extracellular spread ([bioRxiv 555821](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/555821v1.full-text)), and a December 2025 study found individualized connectomes and epicentres beat population-level ones longitudinally ([PMC12690294](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12690294/)). The caveats are significant. In amyloid mouse models the network model was "not necessarily better than a model of spatial spread" ([PMC6331437](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6331437/)), and results depend on which connectome is used ([PMC11211996](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11211996/)). The code is small and scattered. UCL's toolbox has 13 stars and its evidence is a conference abstract ([PMC11716492](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11716492/)). UCSF's MATLAB Nexis has 2 stars and was last pushed in January 2025 ([GitHub](https://github.com/Raj-Lab-UCSF/Nexis)). Below this layer, amyloid-cascade ODE models and the 19-variable, 75-parameter multiscale model are published as equations without confirmed code ([PMC10995318](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10995318/); [PMC12325246](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12325246/)). The quantitative systems pharmacology (QSP) models most relevant to therapy simulation belong to Biogen/Applied BioMath, Certara, and InSysBio. They are evidenced only by posters and vendor pages ([Certara](https://www.certara.com/poster/quantitative-systems-pharmacology-model-for-therapies-targeting-a%ce%b2-and-tau-pathologies-in-alzheimers-disease/)).

At whole-brain scale, **The Virtual Brain (TVB)** is the open workhorse. Its LICENSE file says GPL-3.0-or-later, and it is actively committed to ([TVB LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/the-virtual-brain/tvb-root/master/LICENSE)). Its AD applications are thin, though. Stefanovski 2019 showed that amyloid-driven loss of inhibition reproduces EEG slowing ([Frontiers](https://frontiersin.org/journals/computational-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fncom.2019.00054/full)). Triebkorn's classifier gain (F1 64.34% to 74.28%) came from **only 33 ADNI participants with no external test set** ([bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.02.27.433161v2.full.pdf)). Most of this work comes from one consortium. The Horizon Europe eBRAIN-Health project (about €13M, July 2022 to July 2026) presented TVB "digital twins" of AD patients, but those claims appear only in the consortium's own news, with no outcome-validation paper ([EBRAINS news](https://ebrains.eu/news-and-events/2026/from-digital-twins-to-telemonitoring-ebrain-health-highlights-from-ebrains); [CORDIS](https://cordis.europa.eu/project/id/101058516)). The large "brain emulation" headlines are computing milestones, not disease models. Fudan's Digital Twin Brain realized only **about 0.72% of possible connections** and has no public code ([arXiv 2510.15745](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.15745); [PwC](https://cs.paperswithcode.com/author/dtb-consortium)). The Fugaku mouse cortex is press-release-driven ([UEC](https://www.uec.ac.jp/eng/news/announcement/2025/20251118_7309.html)). The Blue Brain Project ended in 2024, and its successor, the Open Brain Institute, is a freemium, mouse-only cloud platform ([OBI](https://www.openbraininstitute.org/)). Institutional sustainability is also a risk: EBRAINS 2.0's €38M funding phase ends in December 2026, and no successor funding has been confirmed ([EBRAINS](https://ebrains.eu/news-and-events/2024/ebrains-research-infrastructure-secures-eu38-million-in-funding-for-new-phase)).

AI/ML is the area most inflated by leakage. Wen et al. found that "more than half of the surveyed papers may have suffered from data leakage." In their experiment, leakage pushed validation balanced accuracy to 1.00, against 0.75 on a true ADNI test set, and properly evaluated CNNs did not beat an SVM ([arXiv 1904.07773](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.07773)). Yagis et al. showed that slice-level splits inflate accuracy by **29–55 percentage points** and give about 96% accuracy on *randomly labeled* OASIS data ([PMC8604922](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8604922/)). In speech, silence-only segments of the Pitt/DementiaBank corpus give nearly 100% accuracy, which means models learned the recording conditions rather than the disease ([arXiv 2406.07410](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07410)). Biology foundation models fare no better against simple baselines. A Nature Methods 2025 benchmark found that none of five foundation models, scGPT among them, beat deliberately simple baselines at predicting perturbation effects ([PMC12328236](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12328236/)). The existing AD in-silico perturbation work with Geneformer consists of preprints and a thesis that failed at patient-level classification ([Chalmers](https://odr.chalmers.se/items/fcef7244-0cb8-43c0-9d03-f9084d14cdf9/full)). Knowledge-graph and agent systems are real and open: AlzKB has 118,902 entities ([PMC11066745](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11066745/)), PrimeKG and TxGNN are available ([PMC9893183](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9893183/)), and base Biomni is open source. Their AD outputs, though, are retrospective in-silico hypotheses, and the 2026 Alzheimer's Insights AI Prize winners have only press-release validation so far ([ADDI](https://www.alzheimersdata.org/news/alzheimer-s-disease-data-initiative-doubles-1m-prize-competition-for-agentic-ai-solutions-to-accelerate-alzheimer-s-research)).

## Triage: what to build on, what to re-model, what to avoid

The user's definitions are applied strictly below. **A** means open, permissively licensed, peer-reviewed, independently replicated or used, and maintained. **B** means promising but needing vetting or re-modeling before anything is merged. **C** means avoid: retracted, misconduct, unreplicated hype, abandoned, or a license or DUA that blocks use or redistribution in a public repo. Even A items need the reproduction protocol in the final section before the project trusts any number they produce. A means "safe to depend on," not "pre-vetted."

## Every major cohort is DUA-gated, so the repo must ship code, not data

The legal constraint is simple to state. The ADNI DUA says "redistribution of individual participant-level data in any manner is prohibited." It adds that "derived datasets should only be shared through the ADNI-LONI and/or other NIA-designated data repository," and that using AI tools "which may release ADNI participant level data to others, is in direct violation" ([ADNI DUA](https://ida.loni.usc.edu/collaboration/access/appApply.jsp?project=ADNI)). That last clause directly limits contributor workflows: pasting ADNI rows into a cloud LLM is a stated violation. ADNI also requires "ADNI" on the publication byline and review by its Data and Publications Committee before submission. TADPOLE data is downloadable only through the ADNI IDA, so the benchmark's data is ADNI-gated as well ([ADNI TADPOLE](https://adni.loni.usc.edu/tadpole-challenge-dataset-available/)). The AD Knowledge Portal forbids redistributing controlled individual-level data "in any form (raw, processed, derived)" ([AD Portal DUC](https://help.adknowledgeportal.org/apd/Data-Use-Certificates.2623373330.html)). Code licenses matter as much as data terms. GPL-3.0 code (TVB, kde_ebm, BrainPy) makes any distributed combined work GPL, so a permissively licensed core must keep those integrations in separately licensed modules ([TVB LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/the-virtual-brain/tvb-root/master/LICENSE)). AGPL-3.0 (nmed2024) extends copyleft to network use. BSD-3-Clause-Clear (Leaspy) explicitly withholds patent rights. CC BY-NC, NC-SA, and NC-ND licenses (pyment, neuromaps, BrainLM), Mass General Brigham's non-commercial terms (BrainIAC), and AlphaGenome's ban on training other models all prevent inclusion under an OSI license. Repos with no license at all (POYO, flywire_annotations, SuStaInMatlab) confer no rights. The Allen Institute's terms, and AllenSDK's clause against commercial redistribution, mean SEA-AD and Allen files must keep their upstream terms and never be relabeled under the project's license. Two availability risks also argue for pinning everything. ADNI's site and SEA-AD's download page both display a banner saying they are "under review for potential modification in compliance with Administration directives" ([ADNI](https://adni.loni.usc.edu/data-samples/adni-data/); [SEA-AD](https://brain-map.org/explore/seattle-alzheimers-disease/seattle-alzheimers-disease-brain-cell-atlas-download)), and EBRAINS' funded phase ends in December 2026. This is a synthesis of the notes, not legal advice. Have an institution's tech-transfer or open-source office confirm the licensing plan.

## Only two drugs clear the evidence bar, and nothing reverses disease

The clinical record sets a low ceiling for the project's goals. Grade A here means replicated or adequately powered RCTs on a clinical endpoint. Grade B means a single RCT, a subgroup result, a biomarker-only result, or a strong natural experiment. Grade C means small, open-label, observational, or mechanistic evidence. Grade D means uncontrolled, animal-only, or regulator-rejected. Under that scale, lecanemab and donanemab are the only disease-modifying agents rated A. Their effects are modest: **−0.45 and −0.67 CDR-SB points over 18 months** ([PMC review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11963282/)). ARIA-E reaches **32.6% in APOE4 homozygotes** on lecanemab ([PMC table](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12184013/table/tbl0003)). The strongest lesson of 2025–26 is that **biomarker improvement does not reliably mean clinical benefit**. Oral semaglutide improved biomarkers but showed no CDR-SB slowing in 3,808 patients ([Alzheimer Europe](https://www.alzheimer-europe.org/news/novo-nordisk-announces-topline-results-evoke-and-evoke-trials-semaglutide-early-ad)), even though EHR studies had associated it with 40–70% lower AD risk ([Psychiatric News](https://alert.psychnews.org/2024/10/semaglutide-linked-to-lower-risk-of.html)). Diranersen missed its primary endpoint, and its best cognitive signal came at the *lowest* dose ([Biogen](https://investors.biogen.com/news-releases/news-release-details/topline-results-phase-2-celia-study-diranersen-biib080-first)).

The project's charter should therefore say "prevent, slow, and understand," not "reverse." Today's realistic targets are delayed onset, slowed progression, and better risk and benefit-versus-harm prediction. The amyloid hypothesis as a whole does not depend on the retracted Aβ*56 paper, because it rests on independent genetic and antibody-trial evidence. Any model parameter that traces specifically to Aβ*56 oligomer biology or to Masliah-authored data, however, must be re-derived ([Revista Pesquisa FAPESP](https://revistapesquisa.fapesp.br/en/detours-in-the-labyrinth-of-alzheimers-disease/); [Fierce Biotech](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/research/head-national-institute-aging-neuroscience-division-eliezer-masliah-found-have-committed)).

## Six gaps a new open project can genuinely fill

The landscape points to specific niches where an independent, public, unaffiliated project adds value without needing controlled data or wet labs. The first and strongest is a **reference, null-benchmarked network-spreading library**. Spreading models are the most reproducible mechanistic layer, but the code is fragmented (13-star and 2-star repos, MATLAB, dormant forks) and the newest toolbox's evidence is an abstract. A permissively licensed Python package would fill this gap. It should implement NDM, FKPP, amyloid-weighted FKPP, and SIR behind one API. It should always report fits against Euclidean-distance, degree-preserving rewired, and spatial-autocorrelation nulls, which the mouse and connectome-choice critiques show are decisive ([PMC6331437](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6331437/); [PMC11211996](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11211996/)). And it should link SuStaIn subtypes to subtype-specific epicentres. That alone would be a real contribution.

The second gap is **openly redistributable synthetic benchmark cohorts**. No synthetic ADNI or TADPOLE equivalent can be shared, and training a generator on ADNI arguably creates "derived datasets" under its DUA. Generating cohorts from published aggregate statistics, with known ground-truth event orderings, subtypes, and spreading parameters, gives the project public continuous integration, teaching material, and a way to test whether pySuStaIn, kde_ebm, Leaspy, or a spreading model recovers the truth ([medRxiv precedent](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.02.05.24302140.full.pdf)). The third gap is **leakage-proof evaluation as code**: subject-level, site-aware splits enforced by tests, mandatory naive baselines (last value, linear mixed model, hippocampal volume, age plus APOE), calibration reports, and the red-flag checklist turned into a linter. The literature's inflation problem, from Wen's "more than half" estimate to the longitudinal leakage that inflated a corrected AUC of 0.859 to 0.934 ([arXiv 2607.17442](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.17442)), is exactly what an open harness can prevent.

The fourth gap is **biomarker-to-clinical translation and benefit/risk modeling from published aggregate trial data**. The semaglutide and diranersen disconnects and the APOE-dependent ARIA rates are all public at the aggregate level. Industry QSP models covering this ground are closed. An open, transparent model of how biomarker change maps onto CDR-SB, and of ARIA risk by genotype, would serve clinicians and trialists without touching DUA data. The fifth gap is **integrity and provenance infrastructure**. No maintained "awesome-alzheimers" list was found ([ecosyste.ms](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2Fbintangyosua%2Falzheimer)), nobody has scanned AlzPED, Agora, or AlzKB for retracted or Masliah-flagged sources, and no versioned, machine-readable pipeline tracker was confirmed. A curated list, a retraction-filtered build of AlzKB or PrimeKG, and evidence-tier labels on every claim are low-barrier, high-visibility first contributions. The sixth gap is an **open SBML re-implementation of the 19-variable multiscale ODE model**, with identifiability analysis and a parameter-provenance audit. It would put a credible mechanistic AD model into BioModels-style form where none has been confirmed.

## A proportionate starting structure for grantbaxley/alzheimer-research

The repository should encode the vetting and data policies as files and tests from the first commit, so skepticism is enforced by the structure rather than left to good intentions. A minimal layout:

```
alzheimer-research/
├── LICENSE                     # Apache-2.0 for core code (patent grant; GPL-3 compatible one-way)
├── LICENSES/                   # per-directory licenses (e.g., GPL-3.0 for integrations/tvb)
├── DATA_LICENSES.md            # upstream terms for every external dataset; never relabeled
├── POLICY/
│   ├── VETTING.md              # triage A/B/C rules, reproduction protocol, evidence tiers
│   ├── DATA_ACCESS.md          # BYO-data rule, DUA clauses, LLM/AI-tool prohibition
│   └── INTEGRITY.md            # retraction/PubPeer checks, watchlist (Aβ*56, Masliah, simufilam)
├── registry/                   # one YAML provenance record per imported model/dataset/claim
│   └── pysustain.yaml          # source URL, commit SHA, SPDX license (from LICENSE file),
│                               # peer review, replication evidence, retraction check date,
│                               # triage grade, reproduction result, reviewer
├── awesome/                    # curated, graded list of open AD resources (first public deliverable)
├── synthetic/                  # generators from published aggregate stats; known ground truth
├── models/
│   ├── progression/            # wrappers + re-fits: pySuStaIn, kde_ebm (GPL, isolated), Leaspy
│   ├── spreading/              # reference NDM/FKPP/weighted-FKPP/SIR with null models
│   └── mechanistic/            # SBML re-implementations (multiscale ODE) + identifiability
├── integrations/
│   └── tvb/                    # GPL-3.0-or-later module, separately licensed
├── loaders/                    # ADNI/OASIS/NACC/SEA-AD readers that require the user's own data
├── eval/                       # subject-level splits, baselines, calibration, leakage linter
├── evidence/                   # graded claim table (A/B/C/D) with sources; trial tracker
└── .github/workflows/          # CI on synthetic + CC0 data only; license and data-leak scanners
```

The vetting policy should require the reproduction protocol the scouts converged on before any item moves from B to A inside the project. That means six steps: pin the code version and container; reproduce one published figure or table exactly; refit on a cohort the authors did not use; compare against naive baselines and null models; report calibration and uncertainty; and confirm the license from the LICENSE file and the DUA from the primary source. Every claim in `evidence/` carries a design tier (in-silico, preclinical, observational, RCT) and a retraction-check date. Anything traced to Lesné's Aβ*56 work, Masliah-authored papers, or simufilam is excluded unless independently replicated. The data-access policy should be absolute and checked mechanically. No participant-level or per-subject derived data from any DUA cohort may be committed. Loaders read only from user-supplied paths. CI runs only on synthetic and CC0 data, with a pre-commit scanner that blocks ADNI/OASIS identifier patterns and imaging files. Contributors must not send DUA data to cloud LLM or AI services, per ADNI's explicit clause. Any aggregate table derived from ADNI is cleared with its Data and Publications Committee before posting. All external files are pinned by version and checksum, given the federal-review banners. Kaggle, Hugging Face, and GitHub mirrors of cohort data are banned outright. Licensing should follow the same separation: Apache-2.0 core, separately licensed GPL modules for TVB and kde_ebm, CC BY 4.0 for documentation and aggregate results, and no non-commercial, no-derivatives, or unlicensed code anywhere in the tree.

## Conclusion

In open Alzheimer's modeling, the most valuable missing thing is verification, not another model. Tools that slice, segment, stage, and simulate brains are free and good. What is absent is an independent, public layer that re-runs those tools against nulls and naive baselines, on data anyone can legally hold, with every claim's provenance and evidence tier attached. TADPOLE's random-guess result on cognition, the collapse of semaglutide's 40–70% observational signal in a 3,808-person trial, and the 549-fold drop in differentially expressed genes when the first AD snRNA-seq atlas was reanalysed at donor level ([eLife](https://elifesciences.org/articles/90214)) all point the same way: the field's binding constraint is overclaiming, not missing code.

That changes the project's competitive position. Lacking access to ADNI or a wet lab is not a handicap for a project whose deliverables are synthetic ground-truth benchmarks, null-tested spreading models, an evaluation harness, and a retraction-aware evidence registry. These are exactly the components institutional groups have little incentive to build. If the first year produces a graded awesome-list, a reference spreading library that reports its nulls honestly, and a synthetic cohort that anyone can use to check whether their progression model recovers the truth, the repository will have done more to help prevent Alzheimer's disease than another 99%-accurate classifier.
