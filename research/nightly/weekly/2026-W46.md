## The week in one paragraph

The week's pattern was **checking our own ideas against what has already been published - and
finding the answer was often already there.** Three ideas proposed a "cheap first test"; in each
case that test turned out to exist, and it was unfavourable (enriching blood-pressure trials for
white-matter damage; a body-clock marker for trial enrichment) or it showed a simpler solution
already works (slower dose escalation, with no biomarker needed, cuts the main side effect of
anti-amyloid antibodies). One fear was resolved in the hopeful direction: repairing myelin looks
more likely to lower amyloid than raise it. And the project ran its **first analysis on real open
data** (resting EEG), which found no detectable early-warning direction at the dementia stage.
Two lessons recurred: large observational signals shrink or reverse under causal checks, and
*how* a treatment is given (dose shape, escalation rate) matters more than the field's trial
designs assume.

## Most promising ideas, ranked (with evidence grade and next test)

1. **reserve-indexed-titration-rate** *(RCT-anchored)* — the antibody side effect ARIA depends on
   how fast amyloid is cleared, not how much: a gentler dose ramp cut it from 23.7% to 13.7% at
   equal drug exposure and equal amyloid lowering (TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 6, 10.1002/alz.70062, **checked**).
   The idea sets the ramp per person from baseline microbleed burden. **Next:** does the benefit of
   slow titration differ by baseline microbleeds in published data?
2. **remyelination-nonmonotone-trial-design** *(in-silico)* — myelin-repair drugs plausibly have an
   inverted-U dose-response (the opicinumab trials), which standard two-arm and dose-trend analyses
   cannot see. **Next:** extend the model to a responder-mixture endpoint.
3. **maoa-dopegal-wrong-isoform-repurposing** *(preclinical; carried over, unchanged)* — the earliest
   tau site has a drug-reachable step that existing trials missed by testing the other enzyme form.
   **Next:** a biomarker target-engagement study design.
4. **drug-target-mr-pipeline** *(observational; carried over)* — strengthened by a new, much larger
   Alzheimer's genetics study (Nature, 10.1038/s41586-026-11206-y; as reported, not yet checked
   whether its summary data are openly downloadable). **Next:** validate on known controls.
5. **natural-pulse-recovery-rate** and **surgery-recovery-curve-stress-test** *(observational)* —
   measure resilience as the speed of recovery after a disturbance. **Next:** simulate how many
   disturbances per person a usable estimate needs.
6. **hypnotic-plasma-ptau-bias** *(small randomised study, preprint)* — a sleep drug moved a blood
   tau marker opposite to the spinal-fluid marker; could misclassify people at screening.
   **Next:** wait for peer review and a p-tau217 result.

## Ideas parked or killed

- **PARKED — wmh-enriched-prevention-trial.** Its proposed first test is published and points the
  wrong way: in the SPRINT trial's MRI subset (759 people), intensive blood-pressure control went
  with *more* cognitive impairment in the half with heavier white-matter lesions (HR 2.36, 95% CI
  1.20-4.66; interaction p=0.09; PMID 40586237, **checked against full text**). Post hoc and small,
  so not proof of harm, but the premise is gone.
- **PARKED — circadian-period-enrichment.** The anchor result (PNAS 2026, 10.1073/pnas.2527236123,
  **checked**) did not adjust for p-tau217, amyloid or baseline severity, has no replication, and
  the marker needs a skin biopsy and weeks of cell culture.

## Ideas weakened, downgraded or corrected

- **vascular-reserve-aria-dosing** — re-scoped from a dosing scheme to a narrower question (does
  any blood or spinal-fluid marker add to APOE4 plus microbleed count?). Slow titration already
  works without it, and the proposed marker measures the wrong class of vessel.
- **remyelination-prevention-window** — evidence grade lowered from RCT to observational: the
  trial evidence was a nerve-conduction surrogate in multiple sclerosis, and the genetic study
  usually cited for "white-matter damage causes dementia" reports no effect on all-cause dementia.
- **distance-to-tipping-point-signal** — a correction: our earlier statement that early-warning
  signals have "no human precedent" was too strong. Amyloid-positive healthy 70-year-olds have
  about 10% more variable reaction times (10.1002/dad2.12076, **checked**).

## Ideas advanced

- **oligodendrocyte-amyloid-source-test** → investigating. The feared conflict (myelin repair
  raising amyloid) largely dissolves: myelin-making cells do produce amyloid but cannot seed
  plaques without neurons, and a myelin-repair drug lowered amyloid in mice (PMID 34746130).
- **early-warning-direction-test** → investigating, after its first run on real data (below).

## New evidence that moved the picture

- Slow titration cuts ARIA at equal efficacy (above) - the side effect is a rate problem.
- No Alzheimer's combination trial uses a design that can measure synergy; the one historical
  factorial trial (1997, PMID 9110909) found the combination no better than either drug alone.
- For seizure-like brain activity, the genetic evidence runs mainly from Alzheimer's *to* epilepsy,
  and three low-dose anti-seizure trials were null on cognition - so it looks like a consequence.
- Real data disagree on which way an early-warning signal should point: ageing brains show shorter,
  not longer, neural timescales (10.1038/s42003-025-07517-x; as reported).
- Major trial readouts were scheduled for the CTAD meeting (16-19 Nov); nothing was regraded on
  announcements alone.

## Modeling and analysis results (with honest limits)

- **Dose-shape trial model:** for an inverted-U drug, a two-arm test at the top dose has ~3% power
  and a dose-trend test ~3%, while a curvature test reaches ~59%; if the drug is truly monotone the
  simple tests win. *Synthetic.*
- **Preventable-fraction model:** a headline "preventable share" of 23% falls to ~6% when the
  causal effect is modest; delaying onset by five years needs risk roughly halved. *Illustrative.*
- **ARIA control model:** at equal exposure and efficacy, reshaping the dose trajectory cut modelled
  ARIA from 55% to 36%. My first two versions of this model were wrong (the numbers saturated, then
  a path-independence error); the logged version states the assumption that makes it work.
- **Resilience-probe model:** variance is easy to see but not specific; autocorrelation is specific
  but invisible at clinic-visit spacing; a disturb-and-recover probe is specific and improves with
  disturbance size. I had expected the probe to be far more efficient; the numbers did not agree.
- **First real-data analysis (open resting EEG, 88 people):** recovers the known EEG slowing in
  Alzheimer's (d = -0.76) but shows no detectable direction in three resilience-type measures (all
  |d| < 0.2 after adjustment; intervals about +/-0.9). Symptomatic, single-site: says nothing about
  preclinical disease.
