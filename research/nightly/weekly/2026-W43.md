# Weekly report — 2026-W43 (first week)

*Six research nights, 2026-10-19 through 2026-10-25. Written for a non-specialist to read in ten
minutes. Everything here is a research hypothesis, not medical advice; nothing identifies any real
person. Claims carry a DOI/PMID. "Evidence grade" runs from in-silico (a model) through preclinical
(animal/cell), observational/natural-experiment (human, but not randomized), to RCT (randomized
trial).*

## The week in one paragraph

This was the loop's first week: a scaffold plus six nights, each attacking the disease from a
different angle (resilience, a red-team of our own ideas, working backwards from reversal, drug
repurposing, trial design, and population prevention). It produced **20 catalogued ideas — 13
active, 3 killed or parked, 4 advancing** — and, as intended, it was as willing to *disprove* ideas
as to propose them. The single most encouraging finding is external and real: a rigorous natural
experiment shows the **shingles vaccine is associated with ~20% less dementia**, and the protection
looks *not* specific to the virus — which, if it holds, points to a cheap, already-available
prevention lever. The sobering counterweight, also real: **no treatment has ever shown improvement
above a patient's own baseline**, we have **no validated way to even measure such improvement** in a
short trial, and the headline "45% of dementia is preventable" is an observational upper bound that
shrinks sharply when you demand causal evidence. One night's modeling disproved one of our own prior
ideas. That is the loop working.

## Most promising ideas, ranked

1. **vaccine-trained-immunity** (understand/prevent) — *observational / natural-experiment.* Adult
   vaccines may lower dementia risk through general immune "training," not by fighting one pathogen.
   The anchor is a regression-discontinuity natural experiment on the Welsh shingles rollout: **20.0%
   relative (3.5 percentage-point) lower new dementia over 7 years** (Eyting/Geldsetzer, *Nature*
   2025, PMID 40175543 — re-verified against source this week), replicated in Australia and Canada.
   The recombinant vaccine protects as well as the live one despite a different antigen, and
   unrelated vaccines (BCG, Tdap, flu) point the same way. **Next test:** an independent
   (non-originating-group) regression-discontinuity replication, and a head-to-head showing
   antigenically unrelated vaccines also protect. **Caveat:** cohort studies overstate it
   (healthy-vaccinee bias), and no randomized trial with a dementia endpoint exists.
2. **sex-amyloid-tau-window** (detect) — *observational.* Time an anti-seeding therapy by each
   person's amyloid-to-tau interval. This week it survived one objection (the "women's tau is a
   scan artifact" worry was defeated by an artifact-immune fluid marker) but was sharpened by two:
   the interval is confounded with amyloid age/burden, and sex-by-treatment effects are never
   adequately powered in trials. **Next test:** does the interval predict tau accumulation after
   removing amyloid burden and age? (cheap, on existing scan cohorts).
3. **remyelination-prevention-window** (slow) — *RCT (wrong disease) + preclinical.* Myelin damage is
   early in Alzheimer's; remyelination is druggable (proven in multiple sclerosis); yet no
   Alzheimer's remyelination trial exists. Test a cheap generic (prefer metformin) early, with a
   myelin-imaging endpoint. **Next test:** does it move myelin imaging *and* cognition, or myelin
   only? **Caveat:** clemastine, the proven agent, is anticholinergic — itself a dementia-risk
   concern — so agent choice matters.
4. **clearance-threshold-onset** (understand) — *in-silico.* Model disease onset as a tipping point
   where failing clearance crosses the aggregation rate. **Next test (on data):** does a clearance
   proxy predict tau take-off independent of amyloid burden and age? (Our own model this week
   disproved its stronger corollary — see "what did not work.")
5. **repurposing-credibility-gate** / **improvement-endpoint-validation** (methods) — two
   infrastructure ideas: require an active-comparator/natural-experiment bar before trusting any
   observational drug signal, and validate a readout that can actually detect *improvement* (not
   just slower decline), since none exists today.

Also active and worth keeping: a microglia-amyloid predator-prey model (fills a real modeling gap);
WMH-enriched blood-pressure prevention trials; and a go/no-go on whether remyelination raises or
lowers amyloid.

## Ideas killed or parked, and why

- **Killed — detection-vs-biological-resilience.** The claim that women's verbal-memory advantage is
  a mere test artifact failed on its own founding data, and the companion claim that women are
  "under-protected at the synapse" is contradicted by the largest synaptic-marker study (n=3,397, no
  sex interaction; PMID 40164724).
- **Killed — antiviral-hsv-reactivation.** Proposed and killed the same night: the VALAD trial of
  valacyclovir was **negative and significantly worse than placebo** (Devanand, *JAMA* 2026, PMID
  41405855 — re-verified this week).
- **Parked — synaptic-reversal-endpoint.** The marker panel is directionally incoherent for an
  "up = good" readout; anti-amyloid trials push the damage markers *down*, and the protective ones
  have never been driven *up* in people.
- **Parked — fsh-blockade-women.** A single-group preclinical program with contradictory human data;
  hormone therapy (which lowers the hormone in question) is null in randomized trials.

## New evidence that changes the picture (with sources)

- **Shingles / vaccines → dementia.** The strongest new signal of the week (PMID 40175543; Taquet
  *Nat Med* 2024, PMID 39053634 for recombinant ≥ live).
- **Amyloid is not a reliable route to cognition.** Across 14 trials, a 0.1-SUVR amyloid reduction
  maps to **+0.03 MMSE points (95% CI −0.06 to 0.1)** — indistinguishable from zero (Ackley, *BMJ*
  2021, PMID 33632704 — re-verified this week).
- **The prevention headline is observational.** The Lancet Commission's "~45% preventable" is a
  population-attributable fraction; a review of 185 genetic-causal (Mendelian-randomization)
  associations found 73.5% "insufficient," with only education and type-2 diabetes robust (Desai
  2025, PMID 41342141). Hearing aids were null in the ACHIEVE trial's primary endpoint (PMID
  37478886).
- **"Delay onset 5 years ≈ halve prevalence" corrected.** The real figure is **~41% lower
  prevalence** (Zissimopoulos 2014, DOI 10.1515/fhep-2014-0013 — re-verified this week), and it's a
  hypothetical *treatment*, not an existing prevention tool.

## Modeling results, with honest limits

Four small models were run; none is a disease model — each is a clearly-labeled illustration using
the repo's tools or plain arithmetic.
- **Clearance hysteresis (night 3→4).** A spreading model extended with a clearance term showed
  **no "point of no return"**: restoring clearance always reversed the buildup, *even when* pathology
  suppressed clearance. The lesson: in this model family irreversibility can't come from the
  aggregation/clearance balance — it must come from neuron loss (which the model doesn't represent).
  This disproved the hysteresis corollary of one of our own ideas.
- **Ecological regime-shift (night 6).** A different feedback structure (homeostatic self-
  reinforcement) *does* produce hysteresis: collapse at one stress level, recovery only at a much
  lower one. Lesson: whether reversal is possible depends entirely on the feedback structure assumed
  — so "the brain is/ isn't reversible" is not answerable by a model alone.
- **Prevention arithmetic (night 7).** A 5-year onset delay cut lifetime incidence ~39% in a toy
  cohort — the same ballpark as the verified 41% figure — because risk is back-loaded into late ages
  many people don't survive to reach.
- **Limit on all four:** the numbers depend on hand-set parameters and prove only that the tools
  behave sensibly; they say nothing certain about the disease.
