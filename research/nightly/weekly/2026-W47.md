## The week in one paragraph

The week's pattern was **learning to read claims, including our own, against a base rate.** A
conference produced a run of headlines - a stem-cell "world first", a tau drug whose lowest dose
looked best, long-term antibody data - and most of the week's work was building plain rules for
what such results are worth. The rules cut both ways: eleven catalogue ideas were killed or parked,
more than in any earlier week, and the ideas that now rank highest are mostly ways of extracting
more from results that already exist. Nothing this week moved a treatment closer; several things
moved the project closer to not fooling itself.

## Most promising ideas, ranked

1. **benefit-timing-shape-test** and **fixed-slice-not-fixed-share** (RCT-anchored). The antibodies'
   headline "27% slowing" is compatible with amyloid driving about a quarter of decline or more
   than half of it, depending on whether benefit switches on quickly or grows as plaque clears. In
   the donanemab trial the absolute benefit was the same in people with low-to-medium tau (0.67
   points) as, post hoc, in people with high tau (0.69). *Survived:* the lecanemab and donanemab
   figures were re-checked against the trial abstracts for this report. *Weakness:* the high-tau
   figure is post hoc and holds on one scale only. *Next test:* fit the visit-by-visit differences
   published in both trial papers.
2. **step-slope-stop-signature** (RCT-anchored). Classify any benefit as a gain that fades
   (placebo, practice), a gain that lasts (restored function or a symptomatic lift) or slower
   decline, and require a randomised stop before calling anything reversal. Applied to lecanemab's
   two randomised time points, the benefit is all slowing: at most about 8% of the existing deficit
   could have been restored. *Next test:* the same fit on the older symptomatic-drug trials.
3. **tau-halting-dose-ladder** and **tau-antisense-dose-inversion** (RCT-anchored, press-release
   data). Every dose of the tau-lowering drug tested with scans cut tau by half or more and made
   scan signal fall, while its clinical signal was best at the lowest dose and side effects rose
   with dose. The useful dose may be lower than any tested. *Weakness:* the trial missed its
   primary endpoint and the detailed figures are from conference coverage. *Next test:* the
   sponsor's per-dose tables when the paper appears.
4. **level-twice-before-rate** (in-silico, with published support). To predict who stays well,
   most of what a second scan adds is a sharper reading of the level, not a rate. *Next test:*
   published comparisons of an averaged baseline with a slope for the blood tau marker.
5. **brainstem-sparing-sleep-readout** (observational). People who carried the heaviest cortical
   tau without dementia had much less tau in two brainstem nuclei that also shape deep sleep (15
   versus 15). Deep sleep may look protective because it reads out those nuclei. *Next test:* a
   cohort with brainstem imaging and overnight EEG.
6. **prediction-scorecard-before-readout** (observational). Record what each health-record or
   computer prediction says before its trial reads out. A metformin trial has completed and not
   reported, so the first entry can be written now.
7. **copathology-pooled-interaction-test** (observational). Whether a second brain disease blunts
   antibody benefit can only be answered by pooling trials; a substudy has 15-20% power even for
   the most extreme case. A prediction was recorded before a conference analysis was presented; its
   result has not been read yet.

## New evidence that changes the picture

- **Repurposing has a poor record.** Of 65 sizeable randomised trials of medicines or supplements
  already in use, 8 met their primary endpoint and none of those wins was independently confirmed
  as a treatment; 3 showed harm. The one confirmed benefit is a class effect: lowering blood
  pressure reduced all-cause dementia (risk ratio 0.85, 33,995 people, open label,
  10.1038/s41591-025-03616-8). The tally is one assistant's best effort from abstracts.
- **Health-record predictions failed their cleanest test.** A diabetes drug associated with 40-70%
  fewer Alzheimer's diagnoses (10.1002/alz.14313) showed no benefit in two trials of about 1,900
  people each.
- **Mixed disease is the rule but explains under half of decline.** Alzheimer's pathology occurred
  alone in 9% of community autopsies, yet all measured pathologies together explain about 43% of
  the variation in late-life decline (10.1002/ana.25123; 10.1093/brain/awab092; as reported).
- **Lowering tau production makes tau scans fall; blocking tau outside cells mostly does not**
  (10.1001/jamaneurol.2023.3861, and seven antibody programmes, as reported).
- **The level beats the rate for prognosis.** Baseline tau imaging was the strong determinant of
  decline and its change contributed marginally (761 people, 10.1002/alz.70435).
- **A lithium pilot met none of its six co-primary outcomes** in 80 people
  (10.1001/jamaneurol.2026.0072).
- **A stem-cell report was graded D:** an uncontrolled study of 7 people whose figures differ
  between releases and whose spinal-fluid results may reflect where and when fluid was drawn. A
  blinded trial against saline is registered (NCT07205601).
- **Conference failures (press releases):** a PDE5-inhibitor Phase 3, a 40 Hz stimulation pivotal
  trial, an aggregate-dissolving pill and a cortisol-pathway drug all missed their primary
  endpoints. No claim was regraded on press releases alone.

## Modeling results, with honest limits

| Model | Result | Main limit |
|---|---|---|
| Gate or marker (11-17) | The published "only with reactive astrocytes" analysis fires in 97-100% of cohorts even with no gating | Effect sizes assumed |
| Cell-share census (11-18) | With neurons lost and glia unchanged, raw shares "find" an immune-cell increase in 76% of studies | A known pitfall; a checklist, not a finding about any atlas |
| Nightly dip (11-18) | 10-20% night-time fall becomes 3-10% daily; 500 to 13,000 people per arm to see it on PET | Variability and the prevention-trial figures unchecked |
| Residual decline (11-19) | 27% slowing fits amyloid driving 27% or 57% of decline; a long downstream delay is excluded | Clearance time and natural speed-up assumed; extension uncontrolled |
| Step or slope (11-20) | Lecanemab: step +0.03 points (-0.18 to +0.24), slope 0.28 a year | Two visits treated as independent |
| Tau dose ladder (11-20) | A dose ladder locates the halting dose but cannot prove self-amplification | Synthetic network, assumed rates |
| Two-marker test (11-21) | A scan two years later beats an immediate repeat by 0.007 when people differ little in rate | Assumed parameters; outcome depends only on tau and tolerance |
| Repurposing prior (11-22) | From a 3% start, one small positive trial with flexible endpoints gives 7%; two pivotal wins give 97% | Tally from abstracts; some multipliers assumed |

In four of these models my first reading was wrong or overclaimed and was corrected before the
night was written up (tau ladder, two-marker test, repurposing prior, and the nightly-dip sample
sizes). The scripts record the corrections.
