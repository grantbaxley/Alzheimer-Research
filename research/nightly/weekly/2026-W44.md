## The week in one paragraph

This was a week of the evidence pushing back — including on ideas I had earlier called promising,
and on my own models. Nothing new rose to "breakthrough"; several things were honestly **downgraded**.
The headline vaccine idea weakened under a 2026 reanalysis; a randomized prevention trial failed; the
one "reversal" lead (FN1) turned out to be prevention-only; and two of my own models corrected me
(one may have had a key parameter's sign backwards, the other showed a hard ceiling on reversal). The
net picture that keeps recurring across independent nights: **timing is the only robust lever (treat
early), the hardest wall is irreversible neuron loss, and almost every hopeful "reverse it" analogy
caps at "freeze it."** The most useful forward step is not another model but a genuine causal-inference
capability on open genetic data, which I can run.

## Most promising ideas, ranked (with evidence grade and next test)

1. **treat-to-target-proximal-biomarker** *(observational/methodological)* — borrow HIV/oncology
   treat-to-target with adaptive combinations, but aim at tau/p-tau217, not amyloid. **Next:**
   trial-level surrogacy — does lowering p-tau217 track cognitive benefit, or repeat amyloid's hollow
   surrogacy?
2. **reserve-as-redundancy-threshold** *(in-silico→observational)* — reserve as a measured network
   redundancy. The metric prototype works (algebraic connectivity discriminates and falls under
   lesioning). **Next:** does it predict cognition at matched tau in a real connectome cohort?
3. **nonprogressor-rate-plus-synapse / distance-to-tipping-point** *(observational)* — combine a
   synaptic-integrity snapshot with a personal accumulation-rate to flag who won't progress.
   **Next:** does adding the rate term beat the synaptic marker alone (external validation)?
4. **down-syndrome-condensed-window-trial** *(RCT, registered)* — use Down syndrome's near-deterministic,
   time-compressed cascade as the fastest early-treatment test-bed; the first DS anti-amyloid trials
   are registering. **Next:** watch whether ALADDIN reports a tau-phase readout, not just amyloid.
5. **sargramostim-above-baseline-replication** *(RCT, small/fragile)* — the one peripheral-immune
   signal of genuine above-baseline cognitive gain; replicate properly. **Next:** larger RCT with a
   durability readout.

## Ideas killed, weakened, or downgraded (the honest column)

- **Weakened — vaccine-trained-immunity** (was my #1): a 2026 reanalysis (Kivimäki, PMID 42754179,
  **verified against source**) shows the shingles natural experiments imply >100% vaccine
  effectiveness in two of four cases — residual confounding, not a clean effect. The Welsh
  regression-discontinuity (~18%) still stands as least-biased, but the "robustly replicated"
  framing does not. A mechanistic headwind was added too: peripheral immune *training worsened*
  amyloid in the key mouse study (Wendeln 2018).
- **Downgraded — fn1-clearance-restore** from a reversal lead to a **prevention-only** lead: a full-text
  read of the 2026 Nat Aging paper (PMID 42728404) shows it only ever prevented/co-suppressed
  fibronectin, never treated established pathology. The reversal version is untested (now its own
  idea, fn1-staged-reversal-test).
- **No kills this week**, but two self-corrections in modeling (below) functioned like kills of my own
  prior assumptions.

## New evidence that moved the picture (with sources)

- **Crenezumab prevention trial negative** (Tariot, *Lancet Neurol* 2026, PMID 41579901, **verified**):
  treating presymptomatic PSEN1 mutation carriers failed on both cognitive primaries. (Caveat:
  weak-plaque-clearing drug, so a target/timing confound, not a clean refutation of early treatment.)
- **The shingles reanalysis** (PMID 42754179, verified) — see above.
- **Peripheral monocytes do not meaningfully clear plaques** — the "blood monocytes clear amyloid"
  story is largely an irradiation-chimera artifact (Mildner 2007; Prokop/Varvel 2015); microglia may
  not even be rate-limiting for plaque maintenance (Grathwohl 2009).
- **No synaptic readout has ever risen with therapy** — SV2A-PET rises only after sleep deprivation
  (PMID 42335020), never with a drug (ketamine, psilocybin null). A "reversal" trial currently has no
  usable improvement yardstick.
- **Sleep causation is weak** — Mendelian randomization is largely null for sleep traits causing AD
  (PMID 33150399); CPAP and CBT-insomnia improvement trials are null.

## Modeling results (with honest limits)

Four toy models this week; each is a labeled illustration, not calibrated to the disease.
- **Intermittent-clearance (sleep):** at equal average clearance, concentrating it into a short window
  (sleep-like) leaves more burden near the threshold — timing, not just total, matters (a Jensen
  effect of the equation).
- **Identifiability (tau growth):** a nested test shows the growth rate IS recoverable vs pure
  diffusion when genuinely present — but I first used a strawman null and had to correct it; the hard
  confound (growth vs a static vulnerability map) is still untested.
- **Microglia-amyloid predator-prey:** built it; the outcome is a threshold. **Self-correction:** the
  night's biology said my "peripheral immunity boosts clearance" knob may have the *wrong sign*
  (training worsened amyloid), so the model's most policy-relevant assumption is suspect.
- **Reversible-ceiling (cross-domain):** removing the driver recovers the reversible compartment, but
  the achievable ceiling falls the later you start, because irreversible damage keeps banking —
  quantifying why "reverse it like the liver" caps at "freeze" in symptomatic AD.
