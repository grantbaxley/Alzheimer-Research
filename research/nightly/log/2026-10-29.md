## What I found (with sources)

**The cheapest scalable signals are all downstream, and "earliest" ≠ "most predictive."** Plasma
p-tau217 is the front-runner — genuinely presymptomatic and scalable (one blood draw) — but it is a
*readout of pathology, not a diagnosis*, with frequent PET discordance (Palmqvist 2020, PMID
32722745; Ossenkoppele 2025, PMID 40155777; Shir 2026, PMID 42821845). Retina, speech, olfaction,
gait and passive digital are cheaper still but weaker and non-specific. The honest "closest to
cause" verdict: every cheap scalable signal is a *consequence*; the only upstream handles are
genetic (APOE, TREM2). And amyloid is early but weakly prognostic alone (A+T− HR ~2.4 for MCI vs
~19 for A+ with neocortical tau, Ossenkoppele 2022, PMID 36357681) — so the earliest marker and the
most prognostic marker are different things.

**Who won't progress is best flagged by synaptic integrity — and combining signals helps only a
little.** The CSF YWHAG:NPTX2 synaptic ratio adds the most *independent* predictive variance of any
marker (+11–28% beyond tau-PET/NfL; CN→MCI HR 3.0/SD; Oh 2025, PMID 40164724). But multimodal
models beat the best single marker only *modestly* — added plasma markers buy ~1–3% AUC over
p-tau217 + a cognitive test (Khorsand A4/LEARN, PMID 41948540); the combinations that clearly help
and externally validate are rare (one 7-protein + p-tau217 panel, Knight-ADRC→ADNI, PMID 42598783).
Two of tonight's fusion components have *no* human validation yet: tipping-point / critical-slowing-
down signals have essentially no human AD precedent, and connectome-redundancy predicting
non-progression is unsupported-but-open. Passive sleep regularity predicts incident dementia in two
UK Biobank analyses (low SRI HR 1.53; SRI≥70 HR 0.74) but with no head-to-head vs mean duration and
no test against pathology.

## New ideas (all fusions of two catalogue ideas)

Three (detailed in `ideas.yaml`):

1. **distance-to-tipping-point-signal** (detect) = `reserve-as-redundancy-threshold` ⊕
   `clearance-threshold-onset`. A *dynamic* early signal: network reserve + current accumulation
   rate → how close to a personal tipping point. Honest bound: neither component has human
   validation, and p-tau217 already captures most risk.
2. **passive-circadian-early-signal** (detect) = `sleep-regularity-vs-duration` ⊕
   `circadian-period-enrichment`. A cheap wearable sleep-regularity index, validated against the
   lab circadian-period marker and tested against *pathology* (not just diagnosis). Bound: reverse
   causation, shape disagreement, no head-to-head vs duration.
3. **nonprogressor-rate-plus-synapse** (detect) = `synaptic-reversal-endpoint` ⊕
   `identify-autocatalysis-rate`. A two-marker non-progressor test: where you are (synaptic
   integrity) + where you're going (personal tau rate). Bound: the synaptic marker may already do
   the work (1–3% ceiling), and "non-progressor" is lead-time/circularly confounded.

## Modeling

`scripts/identifiability_2026_10_29.py` — can the FKPP growth rate be recovered, or does a
no-growth model fit the same data equally well? **A result, and a correction of my own first
attempt.** My first null had *no network term at all* — a strawman the growth model trivially beat,
which proved nothing (and is itself the night-6 lesson: a weak null fakes identifiability). Fixed to
a nested, network-aware test: FKPP with free growth vs the *same* model with growth off (pure
diffusion). On the corrected test, when the ground truth genuinely has strong autocatalytic growth
(α=1.2, light noise, known seed), the growth term **is** recoverable even from 3 scans — α recovered
exactly, fit ~15–20× better than pure diffusion. So the method is sound in principle. Two honest
limits keep it at "investigating": the harder real-world confound is growth vs a *static
vulnerability map* (not pure diffusion), which this test doesn't cover; and real data add noise,
unknown seed, and misspecification. Synthetic test of the method, not a result about real tau.

## Dead ends and surprises

- **Caught my own bad test.** The first identifiability null was mis-specified and made the growth
  model look trivially identifiable. Recording the correction rather than the flattering first
  number — a weak null is a recurring trap (it fakes both identifiability and "validation").
- **Combining signals is incremental, not transformative.** The appealing "stack cheap signals"
  intuition buys ~1–3% over p-tau217; I built that ceiling into every fusion's kill test rather than
  overselling the composites.
- **No human tipping-point signal exists.** The elegant critical-slowing-down idea has zero human AD
  evidence — so fusion idea 1 is explicitly hypothesis-stage.
- **Earliest ≠ most predictive** is the night's cleanest lesson, now a lens question.
