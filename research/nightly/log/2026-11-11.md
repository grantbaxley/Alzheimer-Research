## The engineering crux: ARIA is rate-limited, not dose-limited

**TRAILBLAZER-ALZ6** (NCT05738486; *Alz & Dementia* 2025, 10.1002/alz.70062) is the key fact: at
**equal cumulative exposure and equal amyloid + p-tau217 lowering**, a gentler early titration cut
donanemab ARIA-E **24% → ~14%** (far more in APOE4 homozygotes), and FDA approved the gentler label.
So ARIA is governed by the clearance **rate/trajectory, not the total dose** — the failure mode is
decoupleable from efficacy. Base rates for context: lecanemab ARIA-E 12.6% (homozygotes 32.6%);
donanemab 24% (homozygotes ~41%); monotonic in APOE4 gene-dose (Zimmer 2025, *JAMA Neurol*,
10.1001/jamaneurol.2025.0065).

## Revisited: vascular-reserve-aria-dosing → INVESTIGATING (downgraded, re-scoped)

The biomarker-gated-**dosing** form is weakened on three fronts. (1) **The controller that already
works is open-loop slow titration** (ALZ6) — it needs *no* per-patient vascular readout, so any
biomarker rule must beat free, static microbleed-count-gated titration. (2) **CSF sPDGFRβ is the
wrong marker**: it reflects deep capillary pericyte injury, while ARIA is driven by
leptomeningeal/cortical CAA that microbleeds/siderosis already image; it's collinear with APOE4 and
single-lab; the field's actual CSF-vs-ARIA proteome study nominated **CHIT1/DDAH1** (innate
immunity), not a pericyte marker (Oosthoek 2025, 10.1186/s13195-025-01799-3). (3) **The only in-vivo
ARIA feedback signal is the microbleed itself** — a lagging *failure* signal, so a closed-loop
controller would be detecting the crash, not preventing it; the feed-forward setpoint already exists
cheaply as baseline microbleed count. **Re-scoped** from a dosing scheme to a **discrimination
question** with a pre-registered kill: does a fluid vascular marker add ΔAUC ≥ 0.02 over {APOE4 +
microbleeds + siderosis + amyloid + MAP}? If not, it's dead. (The decisive test needs banked trial
IPD, which is DUA-gated and off-limits per Rule #1.)

## New ideas

1. **reserve-indexed-titration-rate** (slow, RCT-anchored) — set the per-patient titration *slope*
   from baseline microbleed burden (biomarker-free), a feed-forward refinement of ALZ6's single
   schedule. Kill: no titration-by-microbleed interaction.
2. **bp-cotreatment-aria-brake** (slow, observational) — intensive BP control during anti-amyloid as
   a "protect-while-clear" ARIA brake (Zimmer 2025: antihypertensive use independently lowered
   ARIA-E; a registered intensive-SBP-vs-usual-care RCT now tests it). Kill: that RCT shows no ARIA
   reduction.
3. **factorial-synergy-not-backbone-addon** (understand, observational) — modern AD combination
   trials are all add-on/backbone and **cannot identify the A×B interaction** (no B-without-A cell),
   so they can't measure synergy; the one true AD factorial (Sano 1997, selegiline × vit E) found the
   combination *didn't* beat monotherapy. Kill: across independent pairs, the interaction term is
   reliably ~0.

## Modeling

`scripts/aria_titration_control_2026_11_11.py` — ARIA as an actuator-saturation control problem
(dimensionless). Clearance is linear (dA/dt=−D), binding flux J=D·A, and the ARIA hazard is **convex**
in flux (h = g·J²/R). Honest key point: ∫(D·A) is *path-independent* (= (A₀²−A_tgt²)/2), so a hazard
*linear* in flux could never reproduce ALZ6 — only the **convexity** makes schedule shape matter, and
the real ALZ6 result is the evidence for it. Results: at matched exposure *and* final amyloid, P(ARIA)
falls **55% (front-loaded) → 50% (ramped) → 36% (reserve-set)** purely by reshaping the trajectory;
and the reserve sweep shows lower vascular reserve forces a slower safe clearance (time 30→84) to hold
ARIA at the same level — the redundancy margin a per-patient rule would set. The "reserve-set" arm is
**feed-forward** (safe rate from baseline reserve + the PET-measurable amyloid trajectory), not
closed-loop, honoring the lagging-feedback caveat above. Structural toy; assumed convex hazard; not
calibrated.
