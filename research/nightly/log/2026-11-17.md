## Modeling: the "only with reactive astrocytes" finding cannot tell a gate from a marker

A widely cited study of 1,016 cognitively unimpaired people found that amyloid was associated with
the tau marker p-tau only in those with elevated blood GFAP, a marker of reactive astrocytes (slope
0.34 vs 0.04; 10.1038/s41591-023-02380-x). The causal reading: reactive astrocytes gate the step
from amyloid to tau. `scripts/astrocyte_gate_or_marker_2026_11_17.py` simulates two worlds with the
same amyloid-to-tau effect - one where astrocyte reactivity truly multiplies it (GATE), one where
GFAP merely rises in response to amyloid and tau (MARKER) - and scores how often each analysis
"finds a gate" (400 cohorts of 800 people):

| Analysis | GATE world | MARKER world |
|---|---|---|
| Correlation stronger in the GFAP-high group | 1.00 | 1.00 |
| Plain interaction term (amyloid x GFAP) | 1.00 | 0.97 |
| Interaction, letting amyloid's own effect be non-linear | 1.00 | 0.02 |
| Longitudinal interaction (later tau change) | 1.00 | 0.01 |

- **The published style of analysis fires in both worlds.** A marker with no causal role produces
  the same "only in the positive group" pattern, because GFAP tracks amyloid and tau responds to
  amyloid only above a threshold.
- **Two analyses do discriminate:** allowing a non-linear amyloid term, and a longitudinal test.
- **Limits:** effect sizes are assumed, and the model was given the true threshold; it shows what
  the analysis can be fooled by, not that it was. My first version of the stratified test
  ("significant in one group, not the other") never fired in either world at this sample size, so I
  added the fairer comparison of correlation strength; both are in the script.

The human evidence leans the same way: GFAP's link to tau PET disappears after adjusting for
amyloid PET (10.1093/brain/awab223), and removing plaque with donanemab lowers GFAP
(10.1001/jamaneurol.2022.3392) - so GFAP behaves like a gauge of amyloid. Mouse work disagrees on
the sign of "calming astrocytes": some manipulations lower plaque, others raise it (as reported),
and the field no longer treats reactivity as one state.

## Revisited: clearance-threshold-onset -> PARKED

The idea treated amyloid onset as a tipping point with a point of no return. Human data now
contradict the no-return part: after an antibody clears plaque and dosing stops, amyloid re-grows
at about 2.4-3.4 Centiloids a year - roughly the natural early rate - neither staying low nor
snapping back (sponsor modelling 2.8, conference reports about 2.4, a 46-person abstract about 3.4,
10.1002/alz.078175). That is a single-state system with a lasting production-clearance imbalance:
clearing resets the clock. Isotope-labelling studies show a gradual shift, not a threshold
(clearance about 30% slower in Alzheimer's, PMID 21148344; turnover slowing steadily with age, PMID
26040676). The exact rates are sponsor-modelled or from abstracts. One result could revive a
threshold reading: re-growth *slower* than the same person's pre-treatment rate.
