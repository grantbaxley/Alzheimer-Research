# Night of 2026-11-25: What do the cheap options actually buy?

Inversion lens (work backwards from "cognition improves and stays improved") under a cost limit

## Questions asked

- Of the things that cost under 100 dollars a year, which have randomised or natural-experiment
  evidence, and how big is each effect on one common scale?
- Which of them, if any, is a gain in function, as against a delay?
- Can a small average gain hide a large gain in a few people with something correctable?
- (Revisit) Does the blood marker really rise before the standard tau marker, or did the method
  make it look that way?
- New lens questions added: one scale for comparing options; average gain or responder minority;
  and whether a "rises first" claim was fitted or read off curves.

## What I found (with sources)

**Low-cost options, by strength of evidence** (all as reported by the assistant from abstracts
unless marked).

| Option | Best evidence | Effect | Kind of effect | Product cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Blood-pressure lowering, generic | 33,995-person trial randomised by village; pooled placebo-controlled trials | Dementia risk ratio 0.85 (0.76-0.95), checked; pooled 0.87 (0.75-0.99) | Fewer new cases | Under $10 a year |
| Generic symptomatic drug, in diagnosed Alzheimer's | About 30 trials; stopping trials | +1.05 points on a 30-point screen at six months (checked); lost on stopping | Gain while taken | About $20 a year |
| Shingles vaccination | Natural experiments in three countries | About 20% fewer diagnoses among the vaccinated in Wales | Delay over 5-7 years | About $485 once |
| Daily multivitamin | Three substudies of one trial | +0.07 SD (0.03-0.11), checked | Small gain that held three years | $16-77 a year |
| Speed-of-processing training | One trial; dementia a secondary outcome | 20-year hazard ratio 0.87 (0.72-1.05) overall; 0.75 with booster sessions (checked) | Delay | $96 |
| Lifestyle programmes, exercise | Mixed: two positive, three null | +0.02-0.03 SD a year while running | Slower decline | Not verified |
| Hearing aids | Main result null; one subgroup positive | 0.19 SD over three years in the subgroup | Slower decline | Probably over the limit |

- **Null in large trials:** B vitamins, vitamin D, omega-3, low-dose aspirin, a diet trial.
- **Stopping sedating or anticholinergic medicines:** very low certainty; a 344-person trial built
  to test it has finished and not reported (NCT04270474).
- **Two cautions.** The multivitamin's only independent test, a 12-year trial in male doctors,
  found nothing (PMID 24490265). And a critique of the shingles studies found that scaling from
  "eligible" to "vaccinated" implies reductions above 100% in two of the three countries
  (10.1016/j.bbi.2026.107021, checked); two randomised trials are registered for 2029-2030.
- **None of these is a reversal.** Only the symptomatic drug raises function, and that gain goes
  when the drug stops.

**The blood marker (the revisit).**

- **Where timing can be measured, it rises after the tau marker.** In inherited Alzheimer's, serum
  beta-synuclein starts to rise about 11 years before expected symptoms and after amyloid
  (10.1002/alz.70146, checked); the same paper puts spinal-fluid p-tau217 about 5 years earlier
  (as reported from the full text).
- **In ordinary late-onset disease it has never been timed against the tau marker,** and two
  analyses of one cohort found no rise in amyloid-positive people without symptoms (as reported).
- **The "two decades" came from one cross-sectional study** of 131 people that dated each marker
  by when the patient and control curves separate (10.1002/alz.71860, checked). The marker was
  already 1.75 times higher in patients without amyloid (as reported).
- **No study adjusts for the tau marker** when testing whether it predicts decline, all blood
  results come from one laboratory's method, and its run-to-run variation of about 10% is as large
  as a year's change (as reported).
- **It is not specific:** it rises after stroke, head injury, seizures and in prion disease (as
  reported).

## Modeling

**1. The low-cost ladder** (`scripts/low_cost_ladder_2026_11_25.py`). Each effect is converted to
months of decline or of dementia onset deferred, multiplied by an assumed chance that it is real,
and divided by five years of product cost.

| Option (who it was measured in) | Months if real | Chance real (assumed) | Expected months | Per $100 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Blood-pressure lowering (adults with high blood pressure) | 14 (4-24) | 80% | 11.3 | 22.5 |
| Generic symptomatic drug (diagnosed Alzheimer's) | 7 (5-9) | 95% | 6.5 | 6.5 |
| Daily multivitamin (healthy older adults) | 17 (7-26) | 35% | 5.9 | 3.7 |
| Speed training (healthy older adults) | 12 (0-28) | 15% | 1.8 | 1.9 |
| Shingles vaccination (older adults) | 19 (6-35) | 30% | 5.8 | 1.2 |
| Plaque-removing antibody, for scale (early Alzheimer's) | 16 (8-24) | 95% | 15.4 | 0.01 |

- The firmest cheap options defer about as many months per person as the expensive one, at a few
  thousandths of the cost - but in different people and by different routes.
- The ranking depends as much on the assumed chance of being real as on the size of the effect.
- Limits: the rows are not interchangeable; a hazard ratio is converted to months by assuming
  dementia incidence doubles every five years; the antibody row assumes its slowing persists for
  five years and uses a list price not re-checked tonight; product cost ignores visits, monitoring
  and side effects.

**2. Which marker rises first?** (`scripts/marker_order_artifact_2026_11_25.py`). Two synthetic
markers start rising at exactly the same age in 88 patients and 43 controls. Share of 1,000
simulated studies in which marker A appears to lead by ten years or more:

| World | Curves-separate method | Fitted turning point |
|---|---|---|
| Identical markers | 4% | 0% |
| A is less noisy relative to its rise | 11% | 0% |
| A sits higher in patients at every age | 43% | 0% |

- The method used in the source study invents a ten-year lead in over four studies in ten when
  one marker carries a constant offset, as this one does.
- Counting patients above matched cut-offs shows the same bias; fitting each marker's turning
  point does not.
- I had drafted this model's conclusion before running it and the first numbers contradicted
  part of it (matched cut-offs do not help); the conclusion was written from the results.
- Limits: assumed effect sizes; real trajectories are not simply flat and then rising.

## Revisited: beta-synuclein-ultra-early-signal -> KILLED

The idea claimed an ultra-early signal that rises before the tau marker. Where timing can be placed
against expected onset it rises later, not earlier; in ordinary disease the comparison has never
been made; and the method behind the two-decade figure manufactures leads of that size. What is
left - a marker of synapse or nerve-cell injury that rises with tau - is not the idea. The method
lesson is carried forward as `turning-point-not-divergence`.

## New ideas

1. **months-per-dollar-ledger** (slow, RCT; investigating) - keep every cheap option on one scale
   with a stated chance of being real. Kill: reasonable changes to the assumptions reverse the top
   and bottom rows.
2. **multivitamin-responder-subset** (reverse, RCT) - the small average gain, its step-like shape
   and the null result in well-nourished doctors all fit a real gain in a minority with a
   correctable shortfall. Kill: no larger effect in people with low baseline nutrient levels.
3. **symptomatic-step-positive-control** (detect, RCT) - any "improvement" readout should first
   detect the known gain from the generic symptomatic drug, and its loss on stopping. Kill: a
   candidate readout cannot see what the standard scale sees.
4. **turning-point-not-divergence** (detect, in-silico; investigating) - date a marker's rise by
   its fitted turning point. Kill: a published ordering is unchanged when re-analysed that way.

Also updated: `vaccine-trained-immunity` (critique and registered trials), and the graded claims
(`shingles-vaccine` caveat; new `bp-lowering-dementia` at A and `multivitamin-cosmos` at B).
