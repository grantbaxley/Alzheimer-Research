## What I found (with sources)

**The peripheral immune system is mostly a marker, and "boosting it to clear amyloid" is on shaky
ground.** Peripheral inflammation (IL-6, CRP) associates with all-cause dementia but weakly/null for
AD specifically, and the genetic (MR) causal case is thin. The classic "blood monocytes clear
plaques" story is largely an **irradiation-chimera artifact** — physiological peripheral-monocyte
contribution to plaque clearance is ≈0 (Mildner 2007; Prokop/Varvel 2015), and plaques even persist
when microglia are ablated (Grathwohl 2009), so clearance may not be rate-limiting for maintenance.
Most strikingly, the key preclinical experiment found peripheral innate-immune **training *worsened*
amyloidosis** while tolerance alleviated it (Wendeln 2018, PMID 29643512) — the opposite direction
to the human vaccine→less-dementia signal. Peripheral-immune trials mostly failed (etanercept, IVIG);
the exception is a small sargramostim/GM-CSF trial.

**The synaptic-recovery readout a reversal trial would need has never risen with therapy.** SV2A-PET
*can* rise detectably in humans — but the only convincing within-subject increase is from **sleep
deprivation** (read as pathological, not recovery; Elmenhorst 2026, PMID 42335020). No therapy —
ketamine, psilocybin, anything in AD — has driven a significant within-group SV2A-PET rise, and the
signal sits at the ~3–9% test-retest floor. The one genuine *above-baseline* cognitive gain from a
peripheral-immune agent (sargramostim, +1.45 MMSE within-group; Potter 2021, PMID 33778150) was
tiny, transient, and not a synaptic readout.

## Modeling

`scripts/predator_prey_2026_10_31.py` — builds `microglia-plaque-predator-prey`: a minimal
predator-prey ODE where microglia clear amyloid, exhaust as they feed (self-limitation), and receive
a peripheral-immune reinforcement input `r`. **Result (what the modeling lens asked for):** the
outcome is a **threshold**, not a dial — amyloid either clears or saturates. Across each knob's
range the final burden moves by: clearance efficiency a −0.62, exhaustion q +0.49, peripheral
reinforcement r −0.44 — so exhaustion and peripheral reinforcement are opposing forces on one
boundary, and the flip point depends on parameters the data don't identify. So it yields a
*qualitative* prediction (a reinforcement threshold exists) with a clear falsification, not a number
to trust. **The honest catch, from tonight's biology:** the model's `r>0 helps clearance` assumption
is contradicted by the peripheral-immune evidence (monocyte clearance ≈0; trained immunity worsened
amyloid; inflammation suppresses clearance) — so its most policy-relevant knob may have the **wrong
sign**. Recorded as the model's main limitation and spun into idea 1. Toy ODE, not a brain.

## Dead ends and surprises

- **My model's optimistic knob is probably backwards.** I built a clean predator-prey model with a
  peripheral-reinforcement term that helps — then the same night's biology said peripheral immune
  activation may *worsen* pathology. Rather than bury that, I made the sign the headline limitation
  and a new idea. This is the modeling lens working: a model is only as good as the sign of its
  assumptions.
- **The reversal endpoint problem is now concrete:** SV2A-PET has never gone up with treatment. Any
  "synaptic recovery" trial is betting on a readout shown to rise only from sleep loss.
- **A mechanistic headwind for the vaccine idea** (Wendeln 2018), on top of last night's confounding
  critique — the net sign of immune training is genuinely unsettled.
- **Honest non-finding:** peripheral monocytes do not meaningfully clear plaques; a popular
  intuition, mostly a chimera-model artifact.
