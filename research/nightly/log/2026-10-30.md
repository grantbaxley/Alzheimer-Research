## What I found (with sources, dated)

**A verified blow to my top-ranked idea.** A 2026 reanalysis (Kivimäki et al., *Brain Behav Immun*,
Sep 2026, PMID 42754179 — I verified it against source) quantified the *implied* shingles-vaccine
effectiveness behind the dementia natural experiments: Wales 18%, Australia 177%, Canada 84% and
111%. Two exceed the 100% theoretical maximum, and no matching decline shows up in national dementia
statistics — so the replications that looked confirmatory are implausibly large, consistent with
residual healthy-vaccinee confounding. The flagship Welsh regression-discontinuity (~18%) still
stands as the least-biased single estimate, but the "robustly replicated ~20%" framing I used in the
weekly report does not hold. I've **weakened `vaccine-trained-immunity`** accordingly (details in its
history) — it survives as a hypothesis with a clean falsification, but with lower confidence.

**Presymptomatic anti-amyloid prevention just failed a randomized test.** Crenezumab in cognitively
unimpaired PSEN1-E280A carriers (Tariot et al., *Lancet Neurol*, Feb 2026, PMID 41579901 — verified)
was negative on both cognitive primaries. Caveat: crenezumab is a weak plaque-clearer, so this is a
target/timing confound, not a clean refutation of "treat early." The gantenerumab open-label
"delayed onset" claim (*Lancet Neurol* 2025, PMID 40120616) is open-label, external-control and
early-terminated — grade C, hypothesis-generating, and overstated in lay coverage.

**What DS and ADAD reveal — still amyloid-first, but "remove amyloid → benefit" keeps underperforming.**
The DS tau cascade is *condensed*: p-tau217 and tau-PET turn positive almost simultaneously ~4–6y
after amyloid, vs a longer latency in neurotypical adults (*Brain*, Jul 2026, PMID 41240365) — order
preserved (consistent with amyloid-first), timing compressed by the 3× APP dose. The first dedicated
anti-amyloid trials in DS are now registering (donanemab/ALADDIN NCT06911944; ABATE vaccine
NCT05462106) — the historic exclusion of DS is ending. A new ultra-early blood marker surfaced:
serum β-synuclein elevated ~2 decades pre-symptom, before p-tau217 (*Alz&Dem*, Sep 2026, PMID
42768868, cross-sectional). Resilience mechanism work (APOE3-Christchurch, RELN-COLBOS) points
downstream — glial/vascular, oligogenic, potentially druggable.

**Killed (not a catalogue idea, but recorded):** Down syndrome regression disorder is *not* early AD
tauopathy — p-tau217 identical to controls (*Commun Med*, Sep 2026, PMID 42897209). **No new
registry-worthy open dataset** this window (the DS/ADAD cohorts are DUA-gated; I did not ingest any).

## New ideas

Three, seeded by the frontier findings (detailed in `ideas.yaml`):

1. **down-syndrome-condensed-window-trial** (slow) — use DS's condensed, near-deterministic cascade
   as the fastest, best-powered test of whether *very-early* amyloid lowering prevents the tau phase
   (ALADDIN is the vehicle). Kill test: does lowering amyloid delay the *tau phase*, not just
   amyloid? Safety caveat: ARIA/CAA risk is elevated in DS.
2. **beta-synuclein-ultra-early-signal** (detect) — serum β-synuclein as a presynaptic signal that
   may precede p-tau217 by ~two decades. Kill test: does it precede p-tau217 within individuals and
   add value over age + p-tau217 (both of which dominate prediction)?
3. **resilience-downstream-effector** (slow) — target the shared *downstream* glial/vascular effector
   of resilience variants rather than the variant. Kill test: does mimicking the effector confer
   protection without the variant?

## Modeling

`scripts/redundancy_metric_2026_10_30.py` — prototypes two network-redundancy measures on the repo's
synthetic connectome, the revisit idea's `next` step. **Algebraic connectivity (the Fiedler value)
works**: it cleanly separated a redundant graph (1.145) from a sparse one (0.231) and fell
monotonically as nodes were lesioned (1.145 → 0.471 over 24 removed) — the behaviour a reserve proxy
should show. **My second measure failed**: knockout robustness defined as retained giant-component
*edge weight* did not discriminate (0.636 vs 0.638), because it is dominated by the dense local
backbone both graphs share — it needs a global-efficiency (shortest-path) definition instead. An
honest half-pass: one usable metric, one to rebuild. The real test (does it predict cognition at
matched tau in a human cohort, beyond education and brain volume?) still needs data. Method check on
synthetic graphs only.

## Dead ends and surprises

- **Biggest update: my #1 idea took a hit.** The shingles natural-experiment evidence is weaker than
  I presented in the weekly report; I verified the reanalysis and downgraded the idea the same night.
  This is the frontier lens doing its job — and a reminder that a single rigorous design (Welsh RD)
  plus enthusiastic replications is not the same as robust replication.
- **Anti-amyloid prevention is not a free lunch:** the one randomized presymptomatic trial
  (crenezumab) failed; the celebrated gantenerumab "delay" is open-label. "Treat earlier" remains the
  field's best lever, but early + weak-target still fails.
- **DS is the cleanest near-term prevention test-bed** and it is finally being used — the most
  actionable frontier item.
- **Honest non-finding:** nothing new this window on sleep/glymphatics or remyelination that changes
  the picture; no new open dataset worth a registry record.
