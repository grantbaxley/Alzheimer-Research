## Questions asked

- Ecologists ask whether an invader is the *driver* of a damaged ecosystem or a *passenger* of it.
  What is their decisive test, how often does it work, and what would it look like for the brain?
- When a brain atlas says a cell type "expands", is that an absolute increase or just a larger
  share because neurons left?
- Which fields replaced a whole community or organ, and does any human transplant create a natural
  experiment that separates a cause from a marker?
- Which fields found that the *order* of treatments matters?
- (Revisit) Is any trial testing whether a nightly sleep drug slows amyloid build-up, and could a
  PET trial even see the effect?
- New lens questions added: the removal-experiment question, the share-versus-count question, and
  the order-of-treatments question.

## What I found (with sources)

**Driver or passenger: the test is removal, and removal often fails even for real drivers.**
Ecology's framework: if a dominant invader drives the decline, removing it restores the community;
if it merely fills space opened by something else, removal changes little (10.1890/04-0669). A
later refinement adds the "back-seat driver": a passenger at first that then reinforces the damage
(10.1007/s10530-011-0165-x). The sobering number: in a review of 151 removal studies about 31%
showed no recovery or net harm, sometimes because a second invader moved in, and complete
eradication did no better than heavy suppression (10.1007/s10530-017-1542-x). Recovery after the
stress ends is slow and rarely complete - a median of about 3% a year, with decades-long deficits
(10.1098/rspb.2017.2577, read in full by the assistant; 10.1038/ncomms14163). All ecology figures
are as reported.

**The human "removal and addition" experiments in Alzheimer's so far.** Removing amyloid slows
decline by roughly a quarter to a third - a partial driver. Boosting a supposedly protective
microglial state with a TREM2-activating antibody engaged its target, did not help on any clinical
or biomarker endpoint, did not lower amyloid, and produced brain swelling and microbleeds (company
and press reports of November 2024, which I re-checked; review 10.3389/fnagi.2025.1576020; no
results paper found). In ecological terms that state behaved like a passenger for cognition and a
back-seat driver for blood-vessel harm.

**Replacing the community.** Stool transplants cure recurrent gut infection by replacing the
community, not killing the pathogen (81% after one infusion versus 31% with antibiotics,
10.1056/NEJMoa1205037, as reported). The brain analogue is replacing microglia by bone-marrow
transplant: in eight people with a rare inherited microglial disease, progression halted over two
years (10.1126/science.adr1015, as reported from summaries). For Alzheimer's this stays a thought
experiment: an ordinary transplant replaces only about 1-25% of brain immune cells, about 8% on
average after full-intensity conditioning (10.1182/bloodadvances.2023010409; figures re-checked
against the abstract), and reaching the roughly 90% used in mice needs conditioning too toxic for
older adults. No such trial is registered for any adult neurodegenerative disease (as reported).

**A natural experiment nobody seems to have used.** After a liver transplant, the recipient's
blood APOE switches to the donor's type while brain APOE stays their own (10.1172/JCI115288,
confirmed via reviews). Mice carrying APOE4 only in the liver develop memory and vessel problems
(10.1038/s41593-022-01127-0; one laboratory). The assistant and I each searched and found no human
study of cognition or Alzheimer's markers by donor-liver APOE type. A funded study of cognition
after liver transplant is being assembled (found in a grant listing; not read in detail).

**Continua, and the census problem.** The consensus papers ask the field to drop good/bad labels
for microglia and astrocytes and describe states by several measures at once
(10.1016/j.neuron.2022.10.020; 10.1038/s41593-020-00783-4). The best-known atlas did confirm its
early loss of one interneuron class against spatial imaging in matched donors (agreement R = 0.84,
10.1038/s41593-024-01774-5, from the figure legend). Neither the assistant nor I could confirm
which statistical correction each atlas used for cell-type shares, or find an absolute-count check
for the claimed glial expansions.

## Modeling

**1. A census of shares cannot show that glia expanded**
(`scripts/ecosystem_census_closure_2026_11_18.py`). 400 simulated atlas studies per world, 84
donors each, disease severity as a continuum. Question put to each analysis: do microglia increase
with severity? Cells show the share of studies answering yes and the apparent change.

| Analysis | Neurons lost, glia unchanged | Microglia truly +30% | Oligodendrocytes also lost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Share of nuclei | 0.76 (+20%) | 1.00 (+53%) | 0.97 (+28%) |
| Ratio to oligodendrocytes | 0.02 (0%) | 0.86 (+29%) | 0.75 (+25%) |
| Ratio to astrocytes | 0.03 (+1%) | 0.85 (+30%) | 0.03 (0%) |
| Absolute density (noisier) | 0.01 (0%) | 0.65 (+30%) | 0.03 (+1%) |

- Raw shares report an increase whenever neurons are lost.
- A reference cell type fixes that only while the reference itself is stable.
- Absolute counts are right in all three worlds but miss part of a real change because they are
  noisier.
- Limits: assumed effect sizes and noise; this is a checklist for reading atlas claims, not a
  finding about any atlas. The pitfall itself is well known in microbial ecology
  (10.3389/fmicb.2017.02224) and has a published remedy (10.1038/s41467-021-27150-6).

**2. Does a nightly dip add up?** (`scripts/nightly_dip_accumulation_2026_11_18.py`). A 10-20%
fall in spinal-fluid amyloid confined to the night is a 3-10% fall in the 24-hour average. Plaque
slowing is then 3-10% if deposition is proportional to concentration and 7-27% if it depends on
concentration squared or cubed. People needed per arm to see it on amyloid PET (80% power):

| Slowing | 2-year trial | 4.5-year trial |
|---|---|---|
| 3.3% | 13,253 | 8,248 |
| 6.7% | 3,215 | 2,001 |
| 13% | 854 | 532 |
| 40% (the antibody in the A4 prevention trial) | 91 | 56 |

- For scale, the A4 antibody slowed accumulation by about 40% (11.6 versus 19.3 Centiloids over 240
  weeks) and cognition did not benefit (10.1056/NEJMoa2305032).
- If the night-time fall is made up during the day, or is only a shift in what reaches the spinal
  fluid, the expected slowing is zero.
- Limits: the spread of accumulation (3 Centiloids a year) and scan noise (4 Centiloids) are
  assumed round numbers, and the A4 Centiloid figures are from memory; the assistant that was to
  fetch the published values was stopped. Halving or doubling the spreads moves the sample sizes
  about four-fold, which does not rescue the small-effect rows.

## Revisited: orexin-antagonist-early-window -> PARKED

The idea's stated next step was to check whether a trial with a PET or cognitive endpoint exists.
As reported from the registry scan:

- **No trial of this drug class uses amyloid or tau PET.**
- **Three prevention-style trials are recruiting, all with a blood p-tau ratio as the primary
  endpoint:** suvorexant for 18-24 months in about 120 people (NCT04629547, readout from 2028);
  lemborexant for 6 months in about 200 (NCT06274528); daridorexant for about 12 months in about
  240, with a cognitive composite as a secondary endpoint (NCT07213349).
- **The acute effect is weaker than our entry said.** In the original two-night study
  (10.1002/ana.26641) spinal-fluid amyloid was lower than placebo only after normalising to a
  later baseline, the overall area under the curve did not differ, levels rebounded before the next
  dose, p-tau217 did not fall, and sleep itself did not measurably improve.

Reasons for parking: the PET trial we proposed is a poor test (model above), the informative trials
are already under way, and their chosen blood marker moved opposite to spinal fluid in this month's
preprint (10.64898/2026.11.03.26367196), which is noted on `hypnotic-plasma-ptau-bias`. Re-open if
those trials show blood and spinal-fluid markers moving together, or a kinetic study shows lower
24-hour production. Not answered tonight, because the assistant was stopped: the narcolepsy
natural experiment, comparisons of dementia rates between sleep-drug classes, and safety in older
adults.
