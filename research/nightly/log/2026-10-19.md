## Questions asked

Lens (resilience) under the constraint (focus on women):

- Who carries heavy pathology yet stays cognitively normal, what protects them, and does
  that protection differ by sex?
- Why are about two thirds of patients women — is it biology, or longer survival?
- The female verbal-memory advantage is called "reserve". Is it genuine biological
  resilience, or a measurement effect that just delays diagnosis?
- If synapse preservation (not plaque/tangle load) tracks preserved cognition, can a
  synaptic signal serve as a *reversal* readout, not only a decline marker?
- Where in the amyloid → tau → symptom chain is the sex difference, and does it move the
  window in which an intervention can still work?

New questions worth keeping (added to `lenses.yaml`): see "Tomorrow's open questions".

## What I found (with sources)

**The "two thirds" is mostly prevalence, driven by survival, not a doubled age-specific
risk.** The largest pooled analysis (205 studies, 43 countries) found no sex difference in
age-specific *incidence* of dementia or AD except at 90+, with the prevalence gap tracking
gaps in life expectancy and education (Huque 2023, doi:10.3233/JAD-220724, abstract). Other
cohorts disagree in both directions: COSMIC found HR 1.12 for women (doi:10.1002/alz.12962),
LifeAfter90 sHR 1.89 after age 90 (doi:10.1016/j.lanhl.2026.100882), while UK Biobank found
*men* at higher risk, HR 1.23 (doi:10.1186/s13195-022-01140-2). Selective survival of
healthier men can produce part of a female excess (Framingham, doi:10.1016/j.jalz.2013.10.005;
simulation doi:10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2021.1001) but not obviously all.

**The most consistent biological finding: women carry more tau at the same amyloid.** Seen
at autopsy (more tangles, n=1,453; doi:10.1007/s00401-018-1920-1), on longitudinal tau-PET
in amyloid-positive people (6-cohort meta-analysis; doi:10.1001/jamaneurol.2025.0013), and
in higher plasma p-tau217 at a given amyloid (doi:10.1001/jamaneurol.2025.5670). Caveat worth
holding onto: women show more *off-target* meningeal/skull tau-PET binding
(doi:10.1016/j.nicl.2021.102708), which could inflate part of the imaging gap. The
amyloid-to-tau interval is shorter in women (doi:10.1016/j.tjpad.2026.100622).

**Resilience biology converges on the amyloid→tau coupling step.** APOE3-Christchurch
(R136S) reduces amyloid-induced tau seeding/spread by remodelling microglia, via weakened
APOE–heparan-sulfate binding (knock-in mouse, doi:10.1016/j.cell.2023.11.029); dose matters —
the homozygote delayed onset ~30y but 27 heterozygotes only ~5y (NEJM 2024,
doi:10.1056/NEJMoa2308583). PLCG2 P522R slows MCI decline via lower pTau
(doi:10.1007/s00401-020-02138-6). Both point at suppressing the step where amyloid drives tau.

**Across autopsy/single-cell cohorts, synapse and neuron preservation — not plaque/tangle
load — tracks preserved cognition** (ROS/MAP, SuperAgers, centenarians; e.g.
doi:10.1002/alz.70312, doi:10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2020.31654). The sharpest fluid resilience
marker is the CSF YWHAG:NPTX2 synaptic ratio, ~27% of cognitive variance beyond amyloid/tau
(n=3,397; doi:10.1038/s41591-025-03565-2).

**The central tension:** women have a verbal-memory advantage that *masks* decline
(doi:10.1212/wnl.0000000000003288; sex-specific norms reclassified ~10% of women as missed
MCI, doi:10.1212/wnl.0000000000008467) — yet per unit pathology, pathology is far more often
expressed as dementia in women (odds ~3× men vs >20× women; doi:10.1001/archpsyc.62.6.685).
So "women are more resilient" and "women are more vulnerable" are both supported, at different
stages.

**Hormone therapy does not prevent dementia in RCTs** (WHIMS harm at 65+,
doi:10.1001/jama.289.20.2651; KEEPS and ELITE null near menopause,
doi:10.1371/journal.pmed.1001833, doi:10.1212/wnl.0000000000002980; 2025 meta-analysis null,
doi:10.1016/j.lanhl.2025.100803). The FSH-driver hypothesis rests on single-group mouse work
with contradictory small human studies.

Integrity watchlist: none of the resilience or sex-difference literature scanned rests on
Lesné (Aβ*56), Masliah, or Cassava/simufilam work. The FSH line is flagged as single-group
and unreplicated (not a misconduct finding; PubPeer was unreachable, HTTP 403).

## New ideas

Four, detailed in `ideas.yaml`:

1. **detection-vs-biological-resilience** (understand) — the female verbal advantage is a
   detection artifact, not extra biological resilience; prediction: at matched tau, women
   show equal or *lower* synaptic-integrity markers. Falsifiable with existing tau + synaptic
   panels.
2. **synaptic-reversal-endpoint** (reverse) — a sex-normed synaptic fluid panel (YWHAG:NPTX2,
   NPTX2, NRN1, VGF) used as a *reversal* readout: an upward change as the signal of restored
   function, not just slowed loss. Kill test: do these markers ever rise above measurement
   error in any interventional cohort?
3. **sex-amyloid-tau-window** (detect) — time an anti-seeding therapy by each person's
   amyloid-to-tau interval rather than age; the window may close sooner in women. Tonight's
   model complicated this (below), so it stays `new`, pending the cohort test.
4. **fsh-blockade-women** (slow) — logged **parked**, not claimed: real preclinical program
   (MS-Hu6) but single-group, unreplicated, contradictory human data, and HT (which lowers
   FSH) is null in RCTs.

## Modeling (if any)

`scripts/window_2026_10_19.py` — a synthetic-connectome FKPP run probing idea 3's premise:
does a higher local tau-growth rate shrink the window between tau first detectable in a seed
region and tau becoming widespread? **It did the opposite of the naive intuition.** Raising
local growth (alpha 1.0 → 1.6) pulled *both* first-detection (t≈5.8 → 3.4) and widespread
tau (t≈5.8 → 3.6) earlier together, and if anything slightly *widened* the gap (0.04 → 0.22),
rather than collapsing the usable window. At low growth (0.6) diffusion dominates and the
whole network rises almost simultaneously. Reading: "faster in women" may mean "earlier, and
still detectable before it is widespread", not "no window left" — which weakens the
simple version of idea 3 and sharpens the cohort test. This is a toy model on a synthetic
graph; it shows only that the tool behaves sensibly and says nothing about the disease or
about women.
