## Questions asked

- Starting from the oligodendrocyte: is myelin breakdown an early *cause* or a consequence? What
  repairs it, and what population-scale factors damage or protect it?
- Which modifiable risk factor has real *causal* (not just observational) evidence?
- What would delaying onset by five years actually do to prevalence, and where does that number
  come from?
- New questions invented tonight (added to `lenses.yaml`): is a "preventable fraction" causal or
  an observational PAF — and does protecting a structural substrate (myelin) translate to a
  clinical endpoint, or stop at the imaging marker?

## What I found (with sources)

**Myelin breakdown is genuinely early — but "myelin is the root cause" is unproven.** It shows up
in preclinical AD and even in APOE4-carrier infants (Dean 2014/2017, PMID 24276092, 27842175), and
late-myelinating regions degrade first (Bartzokis). The only *causal* "myelin → amyloid" evidence
is in mice (Depp 2023, PMID 37258678); all human data are cross-sectional co-occurrence. A sharp
contradiction: myelin loss drives amyloid (Depp), yet oligodendrocytes themselves *produce* amyloid
(Sasmita 2024, PMID 39103558) — victim vs culprit, both preclinical. Best honest reading: a
feed-forward loop, not a clean root cause.

**The population-prevention headline largely collapses under a causal lens.** The 2024 Lancet
Commission's "~45% preventable" (Livingston, PMID 39096926) is a weighted population-attributable
fraction from *observational* risk ratios — it only means "preventable" if every association is
fully causal. When you demand RCT or Mendelian-randomization evidence, a review of 185 MR
associations found 73.5% "insufficient" (Desai 2025, PMID 41342141). Specifics that should sober
the messaging: **hearing aids** (ACHIEVE) were *null in the primary endpoint* — the famous "48%
slowing" is a post-hoc subgroup (PMID 37478886); **LDL cholesterol** was newly added at 7% PAF yet
its flagship MR is null for AD (Benn 2017, PMID 28438747); **blood pressure** (SPRINT MIND) cut MCI
but missed the dementia endpoint even on extended follow-up (PMID 39819096); **physical activity**
is a reverse-causation trap (people decline, then stop exercising; PMID 41819119); **smoking** MR
is paradoxically "protective" (survivor bias). The one factor robustly causal in MR is
**education** — and even that may be reserve/ascertainment, not less pathology. The best
*population-scale quasi-experiment* is **air pollution**: cohorts tracking real PM2.5 declines show
lower dementia risk (Wang/Younan 2022, PMID 34983871).

**A correction worth recording.** "Delay onset 5 years ≈ halve prevalence" is misattributed:
Brookmeyer modeled 1–2 year delays; the real 5-year figure is **Zissimopoulos 2014 — a 5-year delay
→ 41% lower US prevalence in 2050** (verified: DOI 10.1515/fhep-2014-0013), and it's a hypothetical
*treatment*, not an existing prevention tool.

## New ideas

Three (detailed in `ideas.yaml`), all starting from the oligodendrocyte and working outward:

1. **remyelination-prevention-window** (slow) — test a cheap remyelination drug *early*, in
   high-myelin-risk people (APOE4 or high white-matter burden), with a myelin imaging endpoint,
   since the AD-remyelination trial gap is real. Sharp safety tension: clemastine is anticholinergic
   (itself a dementia-risk factor), so prefer metformin; and structural repair may not reach
   cognition (the SPRINT lesson).
2. **wmh-enriched-prevention-trial** (slow) — don't conclude BP control fails to prevent dementia
   until a trial *enriched for high white-matter-lesion burden* tests it; first, re-analyze SPRINT
   MIND for a treatment-by-WMH interaction.
3. **oligodendrocyte-amyloid-source-test** (understand) — resolve the victim-vs-culprit
   contradiction as a go/no-go: does promoting remyelination raise or lower amyloid? If it raises
   it, remyelination and anti-amyloid therapy could conflict.

## Modeling

`scripts/prevention_delay_2026_10_25.py` — demographic arithmetic (numpy), not a disease model,
since the lens doesn't map onto the spreading tools. A synthetic cohort (65→100) with age-rising
onset and competing mortality: a 1-year onset delay cuts lifetime incidence ~9% (matching the
classic Brookmeyer order of magnitude), and a **5-year delay cuts lifetime incidence ~39% and peak
prevalence ~27%** — the same ballpark as Zissimopoulos's verified 41%. The mechanism the model
makes visible: onset risk is back-loaded into late ages many people don't survive to reach, so a
few years' delay removes disproportionately many cases. Illustrative only; the toy's exact
percentages depend on hand-set hazards.

## Dead ends and surprises

- **The prevention headline doesn't survive contact with causal evidence.** The most useful finding
  of the night is deflationary: "45% preventable" is an observational upper bound, and the two
  factors that moved 40→45% (hearing, LDL) have a null RCT and a null MR respectively. I did not
  treat the headline as a forecast.
- **A citation correction:** the "5-year delay halves prevalence" line is misattributed to
  Brookmeyer; I fixed it to Zissimopoulos (~41%) and my model corroborates the magnitude.
- **A structure-vs-clinical warning that recurs:** SPRINT slowed white-matter lesions but didn't
  prevent dementia — the same gap as MS remyelination surrogates. It shaped the kill tests for both
  prevention ideas and became a new lens question.
- **Honest gap flagged:** leaded-petrol/water phase-outs are an untapped natural experiment for
  dementia incidence (all the lead literature is childhood IQ).
