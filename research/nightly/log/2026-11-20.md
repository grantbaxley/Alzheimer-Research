# Night of 2026-11-20: Step, fading step or slope - what a real reversal would look like

Reversal-biology lens under the constraint to combine two catalogue ideas into one, revisiting
`identify-autocatalysis-rate`. The earlier reversal night (2026-11-04) asked what could be rebuilt;
tonight asked how anyone would know. A stem-cell report at this week's conference, sent in by the
project owner, is the worked example.

Two research assistants ran in parallel and both finished. I checked the figures that drive
tonight's status changes directly against trial registries and abstracts; those are marked
"checked".

> Research hypotheses, not medical advice; nothing identifies a real person. Claims carry a
> DOI/PMID/NCT. "As reported" marks items from an assistant's report that I did not check myself.

## Questions asked

- What shape does a benefit have over time if function is restored, as against lifted for a while
  or merely preserved - and which published results have which shape?
- Is there a human readout that separates memories that are silenced from memories that are lost?
- When a report says "most patients improved", what would chance and practice alone give?
- (Revisit) Does tau truly self-amplify in people, and how much would production have to fall to
  halt it?
- New lens questions added: fading step, lasting step or slope; what a "share improved" implies;
  and whether the measurement itself moved the marker.

## What I found (with sources)

**No controlled trial has shown a lasting gain above baseline.**

- **Symptomatic drugs give a real but removable step.** Donepezil's advantage over placebo was gone
  six weeks after stopping (PMID 9443470). In a randomised withdrawal trial, continuing the drug for
  a year kept people 1.9 points better on a 30-point screen than stopping it (1.3 to 2.5,
  10.1056/NEJMoa1106668). As reported.
- **The one "above-baseline" signal in our catalogue does not hold up.** The posted results of the
  sargramostim trial (NCT01409915, checked) show the memory-screen score going 17.10, 18.55, 18.00,
  17.10 from baseline to 90 days, in a drug group that started lower than placebo (17.10 versus
  20.75). The fuller cognitive scale worsened on drug (43.2 to 47.7 at 45 days; placebo flat) and
  so did the clinical rating.
- **No trial has raised synapse density on imaging.** The one drug tested with synapse imaging
  showed no difference from placebo (23 people, PMID 38273408, as reported).

**A human readout for "silenced, not lost" exists.** In the free-and-cued recall test, some people
recall normally once given cues (storage intact) and others do not (storage lost). Among 911
amyloid-positive, clinically normal people, 45% had no impairment, 37% and 14% were in the two
cue-rescued stages and about 4% in the storage-loss stages (10.1016/j.tjpad.2026.100641, as
reported from the full text). Higher stages go with more tau and smaller hippocampi and progress
faster (hazard about 1.5, 1.8 and 3.0 times). Nobody has used the test to predict or measure
treatment response, or linked it to synapse imaging.

**The stem-cell report (company: a patient's own fat-derived cells, injected into the brain's
fluid spaces through an implanted port).**

- **Registry facts (checked):** phase 1, open label, 7 people enrolled, one dose of 2, 5 or 10
  million cells (NCT05667649). A randomised, blinded phase 2 against saline through the same port
  is registered but not yet recruiting: 115 people, doses every two months, primary result at 12
  months (NCT07205601).
- **Reported results differ between releases** (as reported): the median cognitive score went from
  53 to 38 in one abstract (10.1002/alz70861_108493) and from 58.8 to 50 in another; group sizes of
  5, 6 and 7 appear; the cognitive gain faded in the second half of the year (press coverage).
- **The spinal-fluid results may be a sampling artefact.** Fluid from the ventricles reads 14-22%
  lower than fluid from the lower back for amyloid-beta 42 and both tau markers, and surgery to
  place a drain raises the tau markers by 140-810% before they settle (PMID 33720890, abstract
  checked). No report says where or when the fluid was drawn. All three markers fell together,
  including amyloid-beta 42, which should rise if amyloid were being cleared.
- **Context:** the only controlled trial of a patient's own fat-derived cells, given into a vein,
  was numerically worse than placebo on every scale (7 per group analysed; registry NCT03117738, as
  reported). A similar cell product given through a port caused fever in all nine people in its
  first study (PMID 34521461, as reported).
- **Graded D** in `evidence/claims.yaml`: uncontrolled and inconsistent. The phase 2 is the test.

**Tau growth (the revisit).**

- **Net local growth is positive.** A network model with regional capacities, fitted to people with
  three or more scans in two cohorts, gives local production of 0.11-0.13 per year once amyloid and
  tau are both present, with transport dominating earlier (10.1371/journal.pbio.3003241; abstract
  checked, rates as reported). An earlier estimate was 0.14 per year (10.1126/sciadv.abh1448).
- **Blocking spread has mostly failed.** Antibodies against tau outside cells did not slow scans or
  decline in most trials; one slowed scans in subgroups without clinical benefit, and one slowed
  growth only where tau already was (as reported; several from press coverage).
- **Lowering production makes scan signal fall.** In the first antisense trial, with spinal-fluid
  tau down about 56%, scan signal was below baseline in every region at 100 weeks (12 people,
  10.1001/jamaneurol.2023.3861, abstract checked). In phase 2, all three doses lowered it (about
  52%, 59% and 65% tau lowering; a reader's digitised conference figures, as reported).
- **Side effects rose with dose:** confusion in 13% at the lowest dose against 25-28% at the higher
  ones and 5% on placebo (conference coverage, as reported).
- **One flagged source:** a mouse paper on the effects of removing tau has a co-author with a
  misconduct finding; it is not relied on here.

## Modeling

**1. Step or slope** (`scripts/step_or_slope_2026_11_20.py`).

- **Lecanemab, from its two randomised visits** (0.17 at 6 months, 0.45 at 18 months,
  10.1056/NEJMoa2212948): a lasting step of +0.03 points (95% range -0.18 to +0.24) and a slope of
  0.28 points per year (0.03 to 0.53). The benefit is all slowing; at most about 8% of the deficit
  people had at entry could have been restored. The two visit estimates are treated as independent,
  which overstates the range somewhat.
- **"k of n improved" without a control group:**

  | Share of untreated people who improve | 20% | 30% | 40% | 50% |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Chance that at least 5 of 6 improve | 0.2% | 1.1% | 4.1% | 10.9% |

  In one older trial 41% of the placebo arm improved at six months (88 of 215, PMID 11110737, as
  reported). The count alone is weak evidence; the size of each person's change against the test's
  retest noise (about 7 points, PMID 9309486, as reported) is what matters, and it was not released.
- **What "share improved" implies about the average:**

  | Report | Share improved | Implied average change |
  |---|---|---|
  | Low-tau group on lecanemab, 18 months | 60% | +0.25 SD |
  | Low-tau group on placebo, 18 months | 28% | -0.58 SD |
  | Low-tau group on lecanemab, 3 years (21 of 41) | 51% | +0.03 SD (-0.36 to +0.43) |

  A group that stands still has about half its members improve. So the three-year figure means
  decline stopped on average in that small, post hoc, open-label group - not that function came
  back. The 18-month figures are from conference coverage, the three-year one from a company
  release.

**2. A dose ladder for tau** (`scripts/tau_knockdown_ladder_2026_11_20.py`, using the repository's
network tools). Two worlds on a synthetic network: tau that self-amplifies with a five-year
doubling time, and tau made at fixed regional rates with no self-amplification. Change in
whole-brain load after 18 months at each cut in production:

| Cut in production | Self-amplifying, clearance cancels 50% | Self-amplifying, clearance cancels 70% | Fixed sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| none | +12.2% | +12.2% | +11.5% |
| 25% | +3.7% | -3.0% | +0.8% |
| 50% | -4.2% | -16.4% | -9.9% |
| 65% | -8.8% | -23.6% | -16.3% |

- In both worlds the response is graded and a ladder locates the dose that halts build-up.
- The ladder does not prove self-amplification. Re-growth after stopping differs in the expected
  direction (accelerating versus levelling off) but only modestly over three years.
- My first reading of this model claimed a threshold pattern that the numbers did not show; the
  script's text was corrected.
- Limits: synthetic network, assumed rates; scan signal can also fall because tissue shrinks.

## Revisited: identify-autocatalysis-rate -> TESTED

The idea asked whether tau's growth term is really positive in people. Others have now fitted it
with the data we could not use, and trials have perturbed it: net growth is positive at about
0.11-0.14 per year. Whether that is true self-amplification or local production limited by
regional vulnerability cannot be settled - the best model fixes regional capacities in advance, as
this idea warned, and tonight's model shows a dose ladder cannot settle it either. It matters less
than the practical number, the smallest cut that halts build-up, which is carried forward as
`tau-halting-dose-ladder`.

## New ideas (each combines two catalogue entries)

1. **step-slope-stop-signature** (reverse, RCT; investigating) - from `benefit-timing-shape-test`
   and `reversible-ceiling-staging`. Classify any benefit as fading step, lasting step or slope,
   and require a randomised stop before calling it reversal. Kill: the rule cannot classify most
   graded claims, or labels a symptomatic drug as reversal.
2. **cue-rescue-recoverable-window** (reverse, observational) - from
   `silent-engram-recoverable-window` and `improvement-endpoint-validation`. Free recall rising in
   people whose recall is rescued by cues is the readout for recoverable memory. Kill: no
   treatment ever moves it more than placebo.
3. **tau-halting-dose-ladder** (stop, RCT) - from `identify-autocatalysis-rate` and
   `tau-antisense-dose-inversion`. Look for the smallest cut in tau production that halts
   build-up; a crude reading of digitised figures suggests about 30-35%, below any dose tested
   with scans. Kill: an arm at 25-35% lowering still accumulates at close to the placebo rate.
4. **half-improved-means-halted** (stop, RCT) - from `fixed-slice-not-fixed-share` and
   `practice-curve-deviation-endpoint`. If plaque removal subtracts a fixed slice, it may halt
   decline in people with little tau. Kill: treated groups still decline in the placebo-controlled
   prevention trials.

## Breadcrumbs from other open projects (standing goal b)

As reported: an openly published AI-agent report (arXiv 2511.02824) proposed that ageing
entorhinal nerve cells lose a membrane-flipping enzyme, expose an "eat-me" signal and are removed
by microglia, and found a matching pattern in early-stage human single-cell data. That is an
untested hypothesis about preventing loss, not rebuilding; it neither confirms nor weakens a
catalogue idea, but it bears on `census-closure-absolute-count-audit` because it too reads cell
loss from single-cell data. No regeneration or cell-therapy hypothesis for Alzheimer's was found
from the other named projects.
