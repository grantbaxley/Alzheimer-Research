## Questions asked

  has anyone shown synergy?
- When does each intervention stop working, and why?
- Starting from sleep: is poor sleep a cause or a consequence, and does improving it change the
  disease?
- New questions invented tonight (added to `lenses.yaml`): has any trial been *designed* to
  measure synergy (a 2×2 factorial) rather than add a drug onto a backbone? And for a sleep/
  circadian variable — does it survive Mendelian randomization and an improvement trial, not just
  an acute-biomarker study?

## What I found (with sources)

**Timing/stage is the best-evidenced idea in the whole field; synergy is not.** Donanemab slows
decline across tau stages but **far more when started earlier** — a modeled 60% vs 33% vs 17%
benefit across progression quartiles (Raket 2026, PMID 42273802). Against that, **no human AD
combination has shown more-than-additive (synergistic) benefit**; the flagship amyloid+tau trial
(DIAN-TU Tau NexGen) gives everyone the amyloid drug and adds anti-tau on top, so it can't even
isolate synergy; the one direct modeling test concluded "clear synergy is not found" (Tyng 2025,
PMID 41009371); and the "clear → protect → repair → rebuild" sequence has **zero** literature
basis. The strongest argument *against* combinations is compounding toxicity: anti-amyloid ARIA
risk is already high (ARIA-E relative risk ~11.7; PMID 42803728), and any add-on inherits that
ceiling for a monotherapy benefit that is itself small.

**The sleep-causal story is shakier than its popularity suggests.** Acute sleep deprivation raises
amyloid/tau biomarkers (robust, randomized — but hours-long, not disease), and poor sleep forecasts
amyloid in cohorts. But **Mendelian randomization is largely null** — little evidence that sleep
traits *cause* AD (Anderson 2021, PMID 33150399) — and the best controlled *improvement* trials are
null: CPAP (APPLES) and CBT-I (Siengsukon 2026, PMID 42298279) showed no cognition or amyloid
benefit. Only the *worsening* direction and an acute suvorexant biomarker drop (Lucey 2023, PMID
36897120, n=38, one night) are randomized. And the glymphatic mechanism that would explain
sleep-clearance is genuinely contested (Smith/Verkman vs Nedergaard/Mestre). Honest verdict:
bidirectional, poor sleep is at least an early marker and plausible accelerator, but "fix sleep to
slow AD" is unproven.

## New ideas

Three (detailed in `ideas.yaml`), all starting from sleep:

1. **sleep-regularity-vs-duration** (understand) — the *regularity/consolidation* of slow-wave
   windows may predict pathology better than mean duration, motivated by the U-shaped duration
   finding plus tonight's timing model. Falsifiable: does a regularity index beat mean duration
   against amyloid/tau PET?
2. **orexin-antagonist-early-window** (slow) — test a dual orexin-receptor antagonist *nightly and
   early*, with PET endpoints, to see whether the known acute biomarker dip becomes durable. Kill
   test: acute-only (no durable change) sinks it — and the shaky sleep-causal premise makes that a
   live risk.
3. **circadian-period-enrichment** (detect) — use intrinsic cellular circadian period as prognostic
   enrichment to select fast progressors for trials, *if* it survives adjustment for p-tau217
   (otherwise it adds nothing).

## Revisited: clearance-threshold-onset

Advanced (stays investigating), two ways. (1) A timing model
(`scripts/intermittent_clearance_2026_10_28.py`) shows the *timing* of clearance matters, not just
its average — which motivated idea 1. (2) The sleep research undercuts any therapeutic reading of
the model's clearance term: the mechanism (glymphatic) is contested and improving sleep has null
RCTs, so the model stays an abstraction, not a "boost sleep clearance to slow AD" claim.

## Modeling

`scripts/intermittent_clearance_2026_10_28.py` — a well-mixed clearance-threshold model comparing
constant clearance vs an intermittent (sleep-like) square wave at **equal daily average**. Near the
threshold, intermittent delivery leaves *more* burden (e.g. mean clearance 0.90 → final burden
0.132 intermittent vs 0.100 constant; 0.95 → 0.066 vs 0.050); well above threshold both clear to
~0. The gaps let the seed regrow — a nonlinear-averaging (Jensen's-inequality) consequence of the
concave growth term. Honest limits: the *direction* (intermittent worse) is specific to
concentrating all clearance into a short window with none otherwise; a gentler duty cycle would
differ. It's a property of the equation, not evidence about sleep — but it does show that "total
clearance" is the wrong single axis, which is the point for idea 1.

## Dead ends and surprises

- **Deflation of a popular story:** "improve sleep to prevent Alzheimer's" is not supported by the
  strongest evidence — MR is null and the improvement RCTs are null. I kept the sleep ideas but
  attached that caveat to each, rather than riding the observational enthusiasm.
- **Combinations don't synergize (so far):** the elegant "stack treatments" intuition has no human
  support and a real added-toxicity cost. I did *not* propose a combination idea on the strength of
  the metaphor.
- **What does hold up:** timing. "Earlier" is the most robust lever in the field — which keeps
  pushing weight onto the early-detection and prevention ideas from prior nights.
