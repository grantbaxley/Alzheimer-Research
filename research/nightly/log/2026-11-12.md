## Revisited: wmh-enriched-prevention-trial -> PARKED

The idea was: before concluding that blood-pressure lowering does not prevent dementia, enrich a
trial for people with heavy white-matter lesions; the cheap first step was to re-analyse SPRINT for
a treatment-by-lesion interaction. **That analysis exists, and it points the wrong way.** In
SPRINT's MRI subset (n=759), intensive treatment was associated with *more* mild cognitive
impairment or probable dementia in the above-median-lesion half (HR 2.36, 95% CI 1.20-4.66; 28/196
vs 12/184 events), no difference below the median, interaction p=0.09 (Reddy et al., J Hypertens
2025, PMID 40586237 - checked against the full text). It is post hoc with about 60 events, so it
does not prove harm, and the same group had *fewer* cardiovascular events. But it removes the
premise that enrichment unmasks a cognitive benefit.

Lesion-enriched trials have also already run. INFINITY slowed lesion growth with no cognitive
difference (PMID 31607143); PRESERVE moved neither imaging nor cognition (PMID 34044580). Neither
was powered for cognition, so this is "consistent but underpowered", not proof. SPRINT MIND's
extended follow-up stayed borderline (probable dementia HR 0.86, 0.72-1.02; PMID 39819096). The one
test that could revive the idea is the same interaction on those extended outcomes; that needs
access-controlled participant data, so it waits for a publication.

## What I found on early signals (with sources)

- **The earliest marker is not the most prognostic.** Plasma Abeta42/40 shifts decades ahead
  (steepest decline about 41 years before amyloid-PET positivity in one cohort, medRxiv
  10.1101/2023.01.12.23284439), but it has a small dynamic range; p-tau217 moves later and predicts
  symptom onset best. Plasma GFAP is not the earliest marker.
- **Most amyloid-positive, tau-negative people stay stable for years** (MCI hazard ratio 2.4 versus
  14.6-19.2 once tau is present; Ossenkoppele 2022, 10.1038/s41591-022-02049-x).
- **Cognitive variability is a real human signal.** Amyloid-positive, cognitively normal
  70-year-olds had about 10% more variable reaction times with the same average speed (Lu 2020,
  10.1002/dad2.12076 - checked); monthly at-home variability tracked tau PET in a small cohort
  (Jutten 2024, 10.1037/neu0000928). This corrects our 2026-10-29 statement that early-warning
  signals have "essentially no human precedent" - a correction note is now on
  `distance-to-tipping-point-signal`. What has *not* been shown is variability or persistence
  rising within a person as a transition approaches.
- **The most interesting finding: the direction of the signal is contested.** Ecology expects more
  variance and slower dynamics before collapse. But ageing human brains show *shorter* neural
  timescales (Wu & Gollo 2025, 10.1038/s42003-025-07517-x), preclinical-model mice show *less*
  behavioural variability (Ehret 2023, 10.1016/j.biopsych.2023.04.009), and frailty activity data
  ran opposite to theory (Rector 2021, 10.1016/j.exger.2021.111341). A naive "rising variance"
  detector may be pointing the wrong way for some signals.
- **Disturb-and-recover probes.** Postoperative delirium is the best natural experiment (recovery
  at two months, then faster decline over six years; Inouye 2016, 10.1016/j.jalz.2016.03.005) but
  is confounded by lower pre-operative cognition. The only purpose-built preclinical "stress test"
  (LASSI-L, 10.1016/j.jagp.2016.02.056) scores recovery within a session. The review found nobody
  measuring recovery *time* after a standard disturbance, repeated at home.
- **The literal ecosystem (gut microbiome).** Composition differs in preclinical Alzheimer's
  (Ferreiro 2023, 10.1126/scitranslmed.abo2984) but there is no external validation, no shown value
  beyond p-tau217, weak causal evidence, and heavy diet and medication confounding. Not catalogued.

## New ideas

1. **natural-pulse-recovery-rate** (detect, observational) - time how fast at-home cognition
   returns after a person's own bad nights and ill days. Kill: recovery half-life adds < 0.02 AUC
   over p-tau217.
2. **surgery-recovery-curve-stress-test** (detect, observational) - score elective surgery by the
   shape of cognitive recovery, not delirium yes/no. Kill: adds < 0.02 C-index over pre-operative
   p-tau217 plus cognition.
3. **early-warning-direction-test** (understand, in-silico) - measure which way timescales and
   variance move before building any detector. Kill: no difference (|d| < 0.2) on open resting EEG
   after controlling for peak frequency.
4. **autoregulation-gated-bp-lowering** (slow, observational) - the cognitive benefit of
   blood-pressure lowering may follow an inverted U across baseline damage; enrich away from the
   most damaged. Kill: the top-lesion third benefits on SPRINT's extended outcomes.

Considered and not catalogued: gut-community volatility (no evidence, costly per person versus a
blood test) and a lesion-location split (SPRINT's regional analysis already shows slowing in
posterior tracts).

## Modeling

`scripts/resilience_probe_2026_11_12.py` - a fluctuating state (Ornstein-Uhlenbeck) in three
conditions: healthy, slowed (recovery three times slower), and "noisy life" (same recovery, more
disturbance). Each detector gets 30 measurements per person. At reliability 0.9:

| Detector | Slowed vs healthy (AUC) | Slowed vs noisy life (AUC) |
|---|---|---|
| Variance, any sampling | 0.88-1.00 | 0.30-0.49 |
| Autocorrelation, dense sampling | 0.82 | 0.78 |
| Autocorrelation, clinic-like spacing | 0.50 | 0.51 |
| Recovery probe, 1-SD disturbance | 0.68 | 0.66 |
| Recovery probe, 2-SD disturbance | 0.83 | 0.80 |
| Recovery probe, 4-SD disturbance | 0.97 | 0.95 |

Variance is easy to see but cannot tell slowing from a noisier life. Autocorrelation is specific
but disappears at visit spacing far longer than the recovery time. A recovery probe is specific and
improves with the size of the disturbance; at 2 SD it is only about as good as dense passive
sampling. I had expected the probe to be far more efficient and the first draft of the reading said
so; the numbers did not support it and the reading was rewritten. The toy assumes slowing happens;
it does not show that it does, and the sign may be the other way.
