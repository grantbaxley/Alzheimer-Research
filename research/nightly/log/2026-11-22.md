# Night of 2026-11-22: Red-teaming our approved-drug ideas against the track record

Red-team lens, restricted to medicines already approved for something else, revisiting
`passive-circadian-early-signal`. I took the three approved-drug ideas that had ranked highest in
the weekly reports and tried to kill them, and compiled the historical record of repurposing in
Alzheimer's to put an honest starting chance on all such ideas. One idea was killed and five were
parked.

Two research assistants ran. One compiled the trial scoreboard and finished. The other, on the
three ideas, delivered all three verdicts and was then stopped by an automated safety filter while
finishing its list of unverified items; I did not resume it or redo its work. Figures marked
"checked" I confirmed against abstracts.

## The scoreboard: 65 trials, 8 wins, none confirmed

Compiled by the assistant from abstracts and registry records - a best-effort tally, not a
systematic review. It covers randomised trials of about 100 or more people, with a clinical
primary endpoint, of medicines or supplements already in use for something else.

| Outcome | Trials |
|---|---|
| Met the primary endpoint cleanly | 8 |
| Met with a caveat (open label, adjusted analysis, biomarker endpoint, one of two primaries) | 4 |
| Harm on the primary endpoint | 3 |
| Missed | about 50 |

- **No win was confirmed by an independent trial as a treatment.** Two were retested and failed
  (an antibiotic pair; an old antihistamine). Vitamin E is mixed. A plant extract "replicated" only
  in its maker's trials. A drug approved only for animals awaits its confirmatory trial.
- **Three trials showed harm on their primary endpoint:** an anti-inflammatory in mild impairment
  (hazard ratio 1.46), hormone therapy (1.76), and an antiviral in early Alzheimer's (PMIDs
  15742005, 15213206, 41405855; as reported).
- **The one confirmed benefit is a class effect in prevention:** lowering blood pressure reduced
  all-cause dementia (risk ratio 0.85, 0.76 to 0.95, 33,995 people, open-label and randomised by
  village; 10.1038/s41591-025-03616-8, checked).
- **Published success rates for the field** are 0.4% for 2002-2012 and 2.0% for 2004-2021
  (10.1186/alzrt269; 10.3233/JAD-215699; as reported). About a third of the current pipeline is
  repurposed drugs; no published figure tracks their success.

**Predictions from health records and computer models have not fared better.**

- The cleanest prospective test failed: a diabetes drug was associated with 40-70% fewer first
  Alzheimer's diagnoses against sister drugs (10.1002/alz.14313, checked), then two trials of
  about 1,900 people each showed no benefit (this project's graded claim `semaglutide-evoke`).
- Only 6 of 38 and 10 of 53 drugs now in Alzheimer's trials were linked to lower incidence when
  compared with drugs of the same class (10.1002/trc2.70193, checked).
- Several widely cited predictions were made after the drug had already failed in a trial, and no
  AI-agent candidate has reached a randomised trial (as reported).
- Human-genetic support is the one feature with general evidence of raising success, by about 2.6
  times across drug development (10.1038/s41586-024-07316-0); repurposing candidates mostly lack it.

**Recent results on drugs still under discussion.**

- **Lithium:** the pilot trial in mild impairment met none of its six co-primary outcomes in 80
  people; one memory test declined 0.73 against 1.42 points a year (p = 0.05); serious adverse
  events 29% against 23% (10.1001/jamaneurol.2026.0072, checked).
- **Metformin:** a 326-person trial completed in April 2026 and has not reported (as reported).
- **No randomised efficacy trial of 100 or more exists** for several drugs often discussed online,
  including the erectile-dysfunction class, one diabetes class and an immune-suppressing drug (as
  reported).

## Modeling: what the track record implies

`scripts/repurposing_prior_2026_11_22.py`.

- **Base rate:** 8 of 65 trials met their endpoint (12.9%, range 6.0% to 21.9%), but 0 of 65 gave a
  win that was independently confirmed (0.8%, range 0 to 3.8%). With no real effects at all, 2-3
  of 65 would "meet" by chance. The model uses 1%, 3% and 5% as realistic starting chances.
- **What a positive trial is worth** (chance the effect is real):

  | Kind of positive result | Start 1% | Start 3% | Start 5% |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Small trial, one pre-set endpoint | 11% | 27% | 39% |
  | Small trial, best of several endpoints or subgroups | 2% | 7% | 11% |
  | One pivotal-size trial | 24% | 50% | 63% |
  | Two pivotal-size trials | 91% | 97% | 98% |

- **What supporting evidence adds, from 3%:** genetic support lifts it to 7.4%; surviving an
  active-comparator test (an assumed multiplier of 1.5) to 4.4%; both to 10.8%.
- My first version started from the 12.9% figure, which counts chance wins as real; the starting
  chances were corrected before any conclusion was written.
- Limits: the counts are one night's tally from abstracts; the multipliers other than the genetic
  one are assumptions; power and false-positive rates are round numbers.

## The three ideas

**1. A reversible MAO-A inhibitor for early tau (`maoa-dopegal-wrong-isoform-repurposing`) -> PARKED,
leaning to kill.**

- The mechanism rests on one research group. No independent laboratory has measured the toxic
  breakdown product in the locus coeruleus and shown it drives tau clipping (as reported).
- An independent industry study found the clipped tau fragment at similar levels in Alzheimer's
  and control brain and only in trace amounts in tangles (10.1186/s40478-019-0831-2, checked).
- The fragment's level in spinal fluid falls in Alzheimer's and tracks late tangle load, so it
  cannot be the early target-engagement marker our test relied on (10.1093/brain/awz346, as
  reported).
- No human signal: a 542-person trial of an inhibitor of the sister enzyme was null, and a drug
  blocking both did not reduce progression in 210 people (as reported).
- Several papers from the originating group carry unresolved public comments that we have not
  read; no retraction or formal finding was found (as reported).
- `perimenopausal-maoa-window` depends on the same mechanism and is parked with it.

**2. Atomoxetine enriched by brainstem imaging (`atomoxetine-lc-mri-enrichment`) -> PARKED.**

- The drug is one arm of a 1,200-person platform trial with clinical endpoints, due around 2031
  (NCT07724132, as reported); the unenriched question will be answered without us.
- Starting the drug was not associated with Alzheimer's risk at 5 or 10 years in a 70,367-person
  health-record emulation (10.1002/trc2.70345, checked).
- A drug of the same class increased tau aggregates and caused memory deficits in mice
  (10.1111/acel.14420, checked).
- The earlier human signal was a 5-6% fall in spinal-fluid tau on an unadjusted secondary outcome
  in 39 people, with no cognitive effect and more side effects (as reported).
- The one study linking brainstem imaging to response, in Parkinson's disease, found that people
  with *less* intact nuclei responded more.

**3. Low-dose levetiracetam gated on silent sleep discharges
(`epileptiform-gated-lev-prevention`) -> PARKED.**

- The gate is not reliably measurable: careful multi-expert review found discharges in 3 of 30
  people with early Alzheimer's and 1 of 30 controls (10.1002/ana.78135, checked), while other
  methods report 18-54% in patients and up to 47% in healthy controls (as reported).
- The unselected trial in mild impairment was null: difference -0.10 points, -0.85 to 0.58, 164
  people (10.1002/trc2.12446, checked). The sponsor withdrew its follow-up trial.
- Genetic studies favour Alzheimer's causing over-excitability, not the reverse (as reported).
- `sleep-eeg-hyperexcitability-screen` rests on the same marker and is parked with it.

## Revisited: passive-circadian-early-signal -> KILLED

This idea fused two others and both have failed their own tests since: the cell-clock marker meant
to anchor it (parked 2026-11-13) and sleep regularity (parked 2026-11-19). Its own kill clause is
met as far as evidence exists: in the one cohort that tested rhythm measures against later amyloid
imaging, regularity did not predict it, and no study shows regularity beating duration. No new
research was needed to decide this.

## New ideas

1. **stored-sample-dopegal-test** (understand, RCT) - use stored samples from the finished
   atomoxetine trial to check, person by person, whether the fall in the noradrenaline breakdown
   marker tracks the fall in tau. Kill: no correlation and no movement in the clipped fragment.
   Only 36 people, so a null would be suggestive, not decisive.
2. **epileptiform-marker-agreement-first** (detect, observational) - before any gated trial, fix
   the detection method and measure reader agreement. Kill: poor agreement, or prevalence under
   about 5% in amyloid-positive people without symptoms.
3. **prediction-scorecard-before-readout** (understand, observational) - write down what each
   health-record or computer prediction says before the trial reads out, to build a measured hit
   rate. Kill: after five readouts it does no better than the base rate.
4. **bp-benefit-vascular-not-alzheimer** (understand, RCT) - blood-pressure lowering is the one
   confirmed repurposing benefit; test whether it sits in people with low Alzheimer's blood
   markers. Kill: equal benefit at high and low marker levels.

Also updated: `repurposing-credibility-gate` (its first criterion is necessary, not sufficient),
`lithium-microdose-target-trial` and `metformin-benefit-concentrates-in-high-wmh` (new results and
watch dates), and the graded `lithium` claim.
