## What I found (with sources)

**No intervention has shown durable cognitive improvement above a patient's own baseline in
symptomatic disease.** Anti-amyloid antibodies slow decline ~27-36% but both arms still decline
(lecanemab, PMID 36449413; donanemab, PMID 37459141). The only scores that *rise* from baseline
are in prevention/at-risk multidomain trials (FINGER, PMID 25771249; US POINTER, PMID 40720610),
but both arms rise and the designs (active controls, 2-year retesting) make practice effects,
not reversed pathology, the parsimonious explanation — "improved more," not proven reversal.

**Reversibility is compartment-specific.** Synapse/spine regrowth is real in mice but has *no*
longitudinal human demonstration (human SV2A-PET shows loss, not recovery; Mecca 2020, PMID
32400950); microglial "reset" to homeostasis with restored cognition is mouse-only (Elmore 2018,
PMID 30276955); remyelination is RCT-proven in *MS*, not AD (clemastine ReBUILD, PMID 29029896);
BBB breakdown is an early human biomarker but no trial shows restoring it helps cognition (Nation
2019, PMID 30643288). The hard floor: ~60% entorhinal layer-II neuron loss is already present at
very-mild dementia (Gómez-Isla 1996, PMID 8699259), and lost neurons are not replaced (adult
human neurogenesis is genuinely contested — Sorrells 2018 vs Moreno-Jiménez 2019). The literature
does **not** support any precise reversible-vs-irreversible percentage; I did not invent one.

**As a control system, the "self-sustaining loop" story is mostly assumed, not measured in
humans.** The strongest human evidence is connectivity-constrained tau spread (Vogel 2020, PMID
32457389) and amyloid accelerating neocortical tau (Pascoal 2021, PMID 34446931) — *coupling*,
not demonstrated autocatalysis. The causal "microglia drive tau spread" step is mouse-only (Asai
2015), and the same human lab reports inflammation has a *dual* effect, complicating a simple
positive loop. No study demonstrates a formal bistable/hysteretic AD onset in prospective human
data; current tipping-point papers are pure models or borrow early-warning theory from
ecology/EEG. And the one simulation that actually *tested* breaking a feedback loop found "clear
synergy is not found" (Tyng 2025).

**A useful check on our own tooling.** The dynamical-systems scan verified that the repo's NDM
(`expm(-βLt)`, normalized Laplacian) and FKPP (logistic growth on the *combinatorial* Laplacian)
faithfully match the canonical published models — Raj 2012 (PMID 22445347), Fornari/Kuhl 2019
(PMID 31615329), Schäfer/Kuhl 2021 (PMID 34335308) — including the combinatorial-Laplacian choice
that keeps x=1 a fixed point. **Caveat to carry:** a good spatial fit (high Pearson r) does *not*
prove the spreading mechanism — Xiao et al. (preprint) argue network dynamics set tau *presence*
while regional vulnerability sets tau *load*. That is directly relevant to how the repo should
describe its FKPP results, and it seeded tonight's idea 1.

## New ideas

Three, detailed in `ideas.yaml`:

1. **identify-autocatalysis-rate** (understand) — fit the FKPP growth term region-wise to
   longitudinal human tau-PET and test whether it is genuinely > 0 (self-amplification) or ≈ 0
   (transport + vulnerability). If the growth term is not identifiable, anti-seeding rationale
   weakens. Also an honesty check on our own FKPP marketing. Modeling-first; kill risk is
   identifiability.
2. **reserve-as-redundancy-threshold** (detect) — operationalize cognitive reserve as a *measured*
   graph-redundancy metric that predicts the tau burden at which cognition falls, instead of an
   education proxy. The gap the control-theory scan explicitly named.
3. **restore-before-the-floor** (reverse) — gate reversal trials on *preserved neuronal substrate*
   (SV2A-PET or entorhinal volume) and read out a plastic compartment, since reversal is
   impossible past the neuron-loss floor. Kill risk: SV2A-PET has never been shown to rise in
   humans — which is itself the blocking experiment.

## Modeling

`scripts/hysteresis_2026_10_22.py` — tests whether the clearance-extended spreading model is
bistable. Protocol (one fixed seed): drop clearance below threshold so pathology establishes, then
restore clearance and check whether burden stays high (hysteresis) or collapses. Run two ways:

- **Fixed clearance:** after a low-clearance episode burden reached 0.40, then restoring clearance
  returned it to 0.00 — the never-ill baseline. **No hysteresis.**
- **Clearance impaired by pathology** (a positive-feedback failure loop, γ falls as burden rises):
  burden reached 1.00 while impaired, but restoring baseline clearance still collapsed it to 0.00.
  **Still no hysteresis.**

Why: the logistic growth term is zero at saturation, so any positive clearance drains the high
state — this model family is reversible *by construction*. The honest lesson for the inversion
lens: the irreversibility of real disease cannot come from clearance/aggregation dynamics as
modeled here; it must come from loss of substrate (neuron death), which the model does not
represent and which the reversibility scan anchors at ~60% entorhinal loss by very-mild dementia.
Synthetic illustration only; a property of the equations, not of any brain.

## Dead ends and surprises

- **Surprise / honest negative:** I expected the pathology-impairs-clearance feedback loop to
  produce a self-sustaining high state. It did not — restoring clearance reversed it anyway. The
  hysteresis I hypothesized last night is not a property of this model family. Recorded, not
  buried.
- **Reframing:** irreversibility ≠ clearance bistability; it = neuron loss. That sharpens
  `restore-before-the-floor` (idea 3) and the window it targets.
- **Caution:** "breaking the vicious cycle" is pervasive in reviews but has *zero* demonstrations
  of durable clinical change; the one real test found no synergy. Treat loop-breaking optimism
  skeptically.
- **Repo-relevant flag:** good FKPP fit ≠ proven spreading mechanism (Xiao et al.).
