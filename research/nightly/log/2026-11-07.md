## What I found (with sources)

**The irreversibility floor is not in the vascular compartment.** Functional vascular states are
reversible — cerebral blood flow (CBF), BBB leakage (DCE-MRI Ktrans; CSF sPDGFRβ pericyte-injury
marker, early and independent of amyloid/tau, 10.1038/s41591-018-0297-y), pericyte-mediated
capillary constriction (reversible on amyloid removal, 10.1126/science.aav9518), and even the WMH
penumbra can partly regress. The hard floor is tissue destruction (microinfarcts, microbleeds,
capillary rarefaction, established CAA) **plus neuron loss** — ~60% of entorhinal layer-II neurons
are gone by very-mild dementia (Gómez-Isla 1996, PMID 8699259). So the **latest point a vascular
reversal could still work is early (pre-symptomatic → MCI), before the substrate is lost.**

**Does restoring vessels reverse cognition? No — it caps at "freeze," like every other route.**
SPRINT-MIND intensive BP control cut the MCI composite and slowed WMH but not dementia, with no gain
above baseline (PMID 30688979; WMH substudy PMID 31408137); exercise raised CBF but the CBF change
did not track cognitive gain (10.3389/fnins.2023.1127065); GLP-1 (semaglutide EVOKE) moved biomarkers
with no CDR-SB benefit (the repo's `semaglutide-evoke`, graded null). Directionality is genuinely
mixed: sPDGFRβ predicts decline prospectively, but Mendelian randomization does not make vascular
risk robustly causal for *pure* AD (it acts largely through vascular/mixed dementia).

## Revisited: regime-shift-trial-signatures → PARKED (vascular mechanism spun out)

The idea's **trial-signature** half fails its own falsification test. Published anti-amyloid
person-level distributions are **unimodal and heavily overlapping with no second mode**
(Espay/Kepp/Herrup, eNeuro 2024, 10.1523/ENEURO.0319-23.2024); donanemab's ~47% "no CDR-SB change"
is a pile-up at the scale floor, not a subpopulation crossing *above* baseline; the dose-response
half is untestable in the single-dose pivotal trials (it was graded in Phase 2); and a
"crossed-back-above-baseline" endpoint still has no validated ruler. As trial design, it is metaphor
dressed as design. I also **softened the idea's cited PMIDs** — they verify as real but are a
network-collapse phase transition and a generic early-warning-signal method, i.e. threshold/EWS, not
a demonstrated bistable basin.

**What survives is strictly the vascular mechanism**, and this is the night's most interesting
finding: the bistability story is *least* metaphorical exactly where the constraint pointed — the
blood vessels. A published cerebrovascular model derives a genuine **saddle-node + S-shaped
hysteresis** from the amyloid→pericyte-constriction→hypoperfusion→amyloid loop (Ahern/Goriely,
*Proc R Soc A* 2025, 10.1098/rspa.2024.0548), with its first arm empirically real in human tissue
(amyloid ~halves CBF, reversibly, 10.1126/science.aav9518). Spun out as `vascular-bistability-hysteresis`.

## New ideas

1. **vascular-bistability-hysteresis** (reverse, in-silico) — a cerebrovascular tipping point whose
   reversal requires *over-correcting* the driver (modelled tonight). Kill: at matched amyloid, the
   vascular readout is unimodal and post-clearance CBF recovery is single-valued (no basin).
2. **capillary-stall-transit-time-readout** (reverse, preclinical) — relieving reversible capillary
   stalls restores flow to surviving neurons (mouse: 10.1038/s41593-018-0329-4); CTH as the human
   readout. Kill: baseline CTH adds nothing to amyloid/tau in predicting decline.
3. **stage-indexed-vascular-point-of-no-return** (reverse, in-silico) — couple vascular burden to
   residual neuron count to map the latest stage a perfusion rescue works. Kill: residual substrate
   adds nothing over vascular burden alone.

## Modeling

`scripts/vascular_hysteresis_2026_11_07.py` — a 2-variable perfusion–damage reduction of Ahern 2025.
Honest results: (1) for **strong** feedback the system is **bistable** in a window of the vascular
stressor (healthy and collapsed perfusion coexist, bounded by two saddle-nodes); for **weak**
feedback it is monostable and perfusion just declines smoothly — so bistability needs strong-enough
feedback and **the model cannot say which regime a real brain is in**. (2) Hysteresis: perfusion
collapses as the stressor rises past the upper node (~0.23) but recovers only below the lower node
(~0.07) — a gap of 0.16, meaning reversal needs the driver pushed ~3× below the collapse point, not
just below the trigger. (3) Even full perfusion recovery is capped by neuron loss. A reduction of a
published model, not a measured basin; the real question (is the brain bistable?) is **empirical**,
not modeling — the test is amyloid-matched vascular bimodality + post-clearance CBF path-dependence.
