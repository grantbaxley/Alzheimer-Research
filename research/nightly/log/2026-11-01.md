## What I found (with sources)

**The single most transferable principle is HIV/oncology treat-to-target — but aimed at the wrong
molecule if it's amyloid.** The cross-field synthesis: hit the disease with *combinations* against a
*quantified, proximal* target and *adapt* (HIV's viral-load model + oncology's measure/combine/adapt).
The cautionary datum is the one transfer already RCT-tested in AD: anti-amyloid antibodies clear
amyloid-PET to near-zero yet only slow decline ~27–35% with no recovery (van Dyck 2023, PMID
36449413; Sims 2023, PMID 37459141) — plaque regression ≠ function regression, because amyloid is an
upstream trigger, not the proximal lesion; tau tracks atrophy. So the borrowable move is
treat-to-target against **tau/p-tau217**, not amyloid.

**But every reversal analogy fails on the same rock:** all five source fields assume a substrate that
regrows or remodels (hepatocytes, arterial ECM, CD4 cells, myelin around living axons, tumor
kill-curves). Symptomatic AD's defining lesion is irreversible post-mitotic neuron/synapse loss with
no regeneration analog — so even a perfect treat-to-target would at best **freeze** disability, not
reverse it. The liver regenerates; lost cortical neurons do not.

**Repurposing scan (symptomatic AD):** nothing has shown cognition rising above baseline. Senolytics
(dasatinib+quercetin) were safe but cognition unchanged in a tiny pilot (SToMP-AD, PMID 38496619);
SGLT2 inhibitors have a consistent *observational* dementia signal but a target-trial emulation was
null (PMID 39186787) — the best near-term RCT candidate, unproven; GLP-1 (semaglutide) and intranasal
insulin failed. Leads sit on the reversible compartments (SGLT2i, senolytics) but none is proven.

**Revisit answered decisively (FN1):** a full-text read of the 2026 Nat Aging paper (PMID 42728404)
shows every fibronectin-lowering manipulation was **prevention/co-suppression** — a constitutive
knockout that blocks fibronectin's rise, or acute 1-day concurrent zebrafish co-treatment; the aged
APOE4 mice with *established* pathology got no FN1 intervention. So FN1 is a **prevention lead, and
its reversal version is untested** — exactly the hepatology question (does the matrix regress once
you remove the driver?) left unanswered.

## New ideas

Three (detailed in `ideas.yaml`), all cross-domain and symptomatic-focused:

1. **treat-to-target-proximal-biomarker** (slow) — adopt HIV/oncology treat-to-target with adaptive
   combinations, aimed at tau/p-tau217 not amyloid. Kill test: trial-level surrogacy — does lowering
   p-tau217 actually track cognitive benefit, or is it amyloid's hollow surrogacy again?
2. **reversible-ceiling-staging** (reverse) — split each patient's deficit into reversible vs banked
   irreversible, estimate the personal reversible ceiling, and set trial endpoints to it rather than
   expecting full reversal. Grounded in tonight's model. Kill test: can the split even be measured
   in vivo (SV2A-PET can't rise)?
3. **fn1-staged-reversal-test** (reverse) — run the missing experiment: lower fibronectin *after*
   established deposition in aged APOE4 mice; does it reverse? The decisive test the 2026 paper
   skipped, framed by the fibrosis-reversal analogy.

## Modeling

`scripts/reversible_ceiling_2026_11_01.py` — a two-compartment toy (reversible pathology P +
irreversible banked damage D), applying the hepatology move (remove the driver) at different disease
stages. Removing the driver always recovers the reversible part (function rebounds), but the
achievable ceiling falls the later you start: treating at an early stage reached final function 0.78,
later stages 0.55, 0.25, then floored — because D keeps whatever it banked. This quantifies the
cross-domain lesson under the symptomatic constraint: "reverse it like the liver" is partly true (the
reversible compartment responds) but the symptomatic stage sets a hard ceiling no clearance can lift,
the exact piece the liver analogy lacks. Structural toy, not calibrated to any patient.

## Dead ends and surprises

- **The FN1 reversal lead is, for now, only a prevention lead** — the decisive paper never treated
  established pathology. Honest downgrade of a claim I had framed as restorative.
- **No repurposed drug improves cognition above baseline in symptomatic AD** — senolytics, SGLT2i,
  GLP-1, insulin all null or unproven. The "borrow a drug from another disease" hope is, so far,
  empty on the improvement axis.
- **Convergent honesty:** the cross-domain scan and tonight's model independently reached the same
  point — borrowed reversal principles cap at "freeze", not "reverse", because AD lacks a regrowing
  substrate. That is the most important, and most sobering, finding.
